# Experiment 03 — Hamiltonian embedding route to a uniform dissipative crossover rate

**Date:** 2026-08-16

## Question

Can the rigorous Hamiltonian uniform-instanton crossover structure be transferred to the present nonlocal dissipative Euclidean action without hand-waving?

The strongest route is not to treat the nonlocal action as fundamental.  The passive linear environment can be represented by harmonic bath coordinates.  The nonlocal kernel is obtained by integrating those Gaussian coordinates out exactly.  This lets us compare the full local Hamiltonian saddle problem with the reduced nonlocal one by Schur complements.

Primary context:

- A. O. Caldeira and A. J. Leggett, *Influence of Dissipation on Quantum Tunneling in Macroscopic Systems*, Phys. Rev. Lett. 46, 211 (1981), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.46.211.
- H. Grabert, U. Weiss, and P. Hänggi, *Quantum Tunneling in Dissipative Systems at Finite Temperatures*, Phys. Rev. Lett. 52, 2193 (1984), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.52.2193.
- H. Grabert and U. Weiss, *Crossover from Thermal Hopping to Quantum Tunneling*, Phys. Rev. Lett. 53, 1787 (1984), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.53.1787.
- J. E. Lawrence, *Semiclassical instanton theory for reaction rates at any temperature*, J. Chem. Phys. 161, 184115 (2024), DOI 10.1063/5.0237368.

## 1. Extended local action

Represent the phase coordinate by \(x(\tau)\) and the linear environment by harmonic coordinates \(y_j(\tau)\).  Schematically,

\[
S_{\rm full}[x,\mathbf y]
=S_x[x]
+\frac12\langle \mathbf y,H_b\mathbf y\rangle
+\langle \mathbf y,Kx\rangle
+\frac12\langle x,Dx\rangle.
\]

The counterterm \(D\) is whatever is required by the chosen passive-bath realization.  The crucial assumptions are

1. the bath is linear and equilibrium;
2. its action is quadratic;
3. coupling to the phase coordinate is linear;
4. the bath quadratic operator \(H_b\) is positive on the Euclidean contour after the usual equilibrium regularization.

The present positive-real two-pole environment is in this class.

## 2. Exact Gaussian elimination

The bath stationary coordinate for any phase path is

\[
\mathbf y_*(x)=-H_b^{-1}Kx.
\]

Substitution gives

\[
S_{\rm eff}[x]
=S_x[x]
+\frac12\langle x,(D-K^T H_b^{-1}K)x\rangle.
\]

The second term is exactly the nonlocal quadratic influence kernel.  Thus the reduced Euclidean saddle equation is the Schur-complement saddle equation of the full phase+bath system.

This is exact for a Gaussian bath; no semiclassical approximation enters the elimination itself.

## 3. Hessian Schur complement

At any stationary trajectory, the full fluctuation Hessian has block form

\[
H_{\rm full}=
\begin{pmatrix}
H_{xx} & K^T\\
K & H_b
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The reduced nonlocal Hessian is

\[
\boxed{
H_{\rm eff}=H_{xx}-K^TH_b^{-1}K.
}
\]

The block determinant identity gives

\[
\boxed{
\det H_{\rm full}=\det H_b\,\det H_{\rm eff}.
}
\]

Because the bath block is trajectory-independent for a linear bath, the pure-bath determinant cancels in ratios between the metastable well, periodic instanton, and sphaleron.  Therefore the determinant ratios computed from the nonlocal action are the Schur-complement determinant ratios of the full Hamiltonian embedding.

This is a strong justification for using the nonlocal Hessian determinant in the physical one-loop prefactor.

## 4. Morse index is preserved

Since \(H_b>0\), Sylvester inertia for a block matrix with positive bath block gives

\[
\operatorname{Inertia}(H_{\rm full})
=\operatorname{Inertia}(H_b)+
\operatorname{Inertia}(H_{\rm eff}).
\]

Thus all negative directions relevant to metastable decay reside in the Schur complement.  In particular,

- one negative reduced bounce mode implies one negative full bounce mode;
- the first-Matsubara crossover of the reduced sphaleron is the full-system crossover;
- no hidden negative bath mode is generated by integrating out a stable equilibrium linear bath.

## 5. Translation zero mode is preserved

If \(\delta x\propto \dot x_b\) is a zero mode of the reduced Hessian, the corresponding full fluctuation is

\[
\delta\mathbf y=-H_b^{-1}K\delta x.
\]

Then

\[
H_{\rm full}
\binom{\delta x}{\delta\mathbf y}=0.
\]

Hence the translation collective coordinate found numerically in the nonlocal problem is the projection of the full phase+bath time-translation zero mode.

This explains why the odd-sector zero-mode overlap in the current determinant calculation approaches unity without an additional bath collective coordinate.

## 6. Consequence for the two-soft-mode crossover

At the sphaleron, the \(n=\pm1\) phase Matsubara modes become soft.  Their bath components are uniquely slaved by the linear relation above.  Therefore the two-dimensional soft plane in the reduced action is the projection of the two-dimensional soft plane in the full Hamiltonian embedding.

Time-translation symmetry rotates the cosine/sine pair, giving the radial normal form

\[
B_{\rm red}=B_{\rm sph}
+\frac12\lambda_1\rho^2
+\frac14g_4\rho^4+\cdots.
\]

The exact quartic soft-plane uniformization derived separately therefore has a direct full-system interpretation, rather than being an arbitrary regularization of a reduced Gaussian divergence.

## 7. What this does and does not prove about Lawrence's uniform rate

Lawrence's 2024 derivation gives a multidimensional Hamiltonian uniform asymptotic rate which, for a smoothly collapsing instanton, contains an erfc-weighted instanton term plus a companion parabolic-barrier term arranged to cancel the crossover singularity.

The embedding argument establishes several prerequisites for transferring that structure:

- the nonlocal saddle is the exact phase projection of a local extended saddle;
- determinant ratios reduce by an exact Schur complement;
- Morse index and translation zero mode are preserved;
- the soft pair is the projected full-system soft pair.

However, a complete proof still requires controlling the continuum-bath limit of the **flux normalization / transition-state term**, not only the Euclidean determinant ratio.  In particular, the rate is a flux divided by metastable population; eliminating a bath in the equilibrium partition function is straightforward, but the unstable real-time flux factor must be shown to reduce to the corresponding dissipative barrier growth rate.

Therefore the current status is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{uniform Hamiltonian structure: strongly justified;}
\quad
\text{complete nonlocal rate formula: not yet proven.}
}
\]

## 8. Next derivation gate

The next object to compute is the parabolic dissipative sphaleron rate in the same environment.  For a linear bath it should factor into

1. the classical/real-time unstable growth rate of the damped barrier mode;
2. the metastable-well / sphaleron stable Matsubara determinant ratio;
3. \(e^{-B_{\rm sph}}\).

The resulting expression must

- reproduce the classical Grote-Hynes/Kramers barrier growth factor in the high-temperature limit;
- have the expected first-Matsubara pole at \(T=T_\times\) before uniformization;
- cancel that pole against the uniform instanton contribution;
- reduce to the existing calibrated Gaussian instanton result well below crossover.

Only after those four regressions pass should `.213-.214` be re-opened as admissible design points.
