# Infinite-Dimensional Passive H2 Audit — 2026-08-09

## Purpose

Close the strongest mathematical objection left by the hostile referee audit:

> The manuscript proves the selected-port H2 inequality with finite-dimensional matrices, while an elastic body has a countably infinite normal-mode spectrum. Does the endpoint bound actually survive an infinite passive modal sector, or is "arbitrarily many modes" only a finite-truncation statement?

The answer is stronger than the previous manuscript wording:

> **For a countably infinite passive Markov modal sector generated by a contraction semigroup with bounded full port coupling, the selected-port H2 inequality holds directly on the Hilbert space. No finite-mode limiting argument is required. If the gravitational port operator is Hilbert–Schmidt, the H2 norm is finite and is bounded by its gravitational coupling trace.**

The classical inertia sum rule supplies exactly that Hilbert–Schmidt condition.

## 1. Hilbert-space passive realization

Let

```math
\mathcal X
```

be a separable complex Hilbert space of internal modal amplitudes, and let

```math
\mathcal U,\qquad \mathcal G,\qquad \mathcal L
```

be the useful-input, gravitational, and other-loss channel Hilbert spaces.

Let the full channel space be

```math
\mathcal Y=\mathcal U\oplus\mathcal G\oplus\mathcal L
```

and let

```math
K:\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y,
\qquad
K=
\begin{pmatrix}
K_u\\K_g\\K_\ell
\end{pmatrix}
```

be bounded.

Take an internal self-adjoint Hamiltonian / classical coupled-mode frequency operator `H` and define, on `D(H)`,

```math
A=-iH-\frac12K^\dagger K.
```

Because `K^dagger K` is bounded and nonnegative, `A` is a maximal dissipative bounded perturbation of `-iH` and generates a strongly continuous contraction semigroup

```math
\mathcal T(t)=e^{At}.
```

This is the direct Hilbert-space analogue of the finite-dimensional completely passive realization used in the manuscript.

The selected impulse response is

```math
h_{g\leftarrow u}(t)
=-K_g\mathcal T(t)K_u^\dagger,
\qquad t\ge0.
```

No assumption of a finite number of internal modes has been made.

## 2. Finite-time passive Gramian identity

The dissipativity identity is

```math
A+A^\dagger=-K^\dagger K
```

in the usual operator/form sense on the generator domain.

For every finite `tau`, the semigroup Lyapunov identity gives

```math
\boxed{
\int_0^\tau
\mathcal T(t)K^\dagger K\mathcal T^\dagger(t)\,dt
=
I-\mathcal T(\tau)\mathcal T^\dagger(\tau).
}
```

Since `mathcal T(t)` is contractive,

```math
0\le
I-\mathcal T(\tau)\mathcal T^\dagger(\tau)
\le I.
```

Define the finite-time selected-input controllability operator

```math
P_u(\tau)
=\int_0^\tau
\mathcal T(t)K_u^\dagger K_u\mathcal T^\dagger(t)\,dt.
```

Because

```math
0\le K_u^\dagger K_u\le K^\dagger K,
```

we obtain directly

```math
\boxed{
0\le P_u(\tau)\le I
\qquad\text{for every }\tau>0.
}
```

This is the infinite-dimensional version of the finite matrix Gramian inequality.

Importantly, **asymptotic stability is not needed for the inequality itself**. Strong stability would make the full-channel Gramian approach `I`; without it, the finite-time full-channel Gramian approaches a positive contraction no larger than `I`, which is already sufficient.

## 3. Infinite-time selected Gramian

The family `P_u(tau)` is monotone increasing in the positive-operator order and uniformly bounded by `I`. Therefore it has a strong operator limit

```math
P_u
=\operatorname*{s-lim}_{\tau\to\infty}P_u(\tau),
```

with

```math
\boxed{
0\le P_u\le I.
}
```

Thus the selected controllability Gramian exists as a bounded positive operator even when `mathcal X` is infinite-dimensional.

This is the exact point that removes the finite-mode-count loophole.

## 4. Hilbert–Schmidt gravitational resource

Assume

```math
K_g\in\mathcal S_2(\mathcal X,\mathcal G),
```

where `mathcal S_2` denotes the Hilbert–Schmidt class.

Equivalently,

```math
\|K_g\|_{\rm HS}^2
=\operatorname{Tr}(K_g^\dagger K_g)
<\infty.
```

Then

```math
K_gP_uK_g^\dagger
```

is positive trace class and

```math
\operatorname{Tr}(K_gP_uK_g^\dagger)
\le
\operatorname{Tr}(K_gK_g^\dagger)
=\|K_g\|_{\rm HS}^2.
```

On the other hand, monotone convergence of the nonnegative trace integrand gives

```math
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^\infty
\|h_{g\leftarrow u}(t)\|_{\rm HS}^2dt
&=
\int_0^\infty
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
K_g\mathcal T(t)K_u^\dagger K_u
\mathcal T^\dagger(t)K_g^\dagger
\right]dt\\
&=\operatorname{Tr}(K_gP_uK_g^\dagger).
\end{aligned}
```

Therefore

```math
\boxed{
\int_0^\infty
\|h_{g\leftarrow u}(t)\|_{\rm HS}^2dt
\le
\operatorname{Tr}(K_g^\dagger K_g).
}
```

This is the selected-port passive H2 cut at the endpoint, now with a genuinely countably infinite state space.

## 5. Frequency-domain H2 form

The preceding inequality already proves that

```math
h_{g\leftarrow u}
\in
L^2(\mathbb R_+;\mathcal S_2).
```

Extend the causal impulse response by zero to negative time. The Hilbert-space-valued Plancherel theorem then gives an `L^2` Fourier boundary value `S_{g<-u}(i omega)` satisfying

```math
\boxed{
\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\|S_{g\leftarrow u}(i\omega)\|_{\rm HS}^2d\omega
=
\int_0^\infty
\|h_{g\leftarrow u}(t)\|_{\rm HS}^2dt.
}
```

Hence

```math
\boxed{
\|S_{g\leftarrow u}\|_2^2
\le
\operatorname{Tr}(K_g^\dagger K_g).
}
```

For finite-dimensional channel spaces, the Hilbert–Schmidt norm reduces to the Frobenius norm and this is exactly the manuscript formula

```math
\frac1{2\pi}\int
\operatorname{Tr}[S^\dagger(i\omega)S(i\omega)]d\omega.
```

Thus the frequency-domain metric itself extends without changing normalization.

## 6. Why gravity supplies the required trace-class condition

For the compact linear-harmonic matter sector of Experiment 02,

```math
\operatorname{Tr}(K_g^\dagger K_g)
=\sum_n\kappa_{g,n}.
```

The classical quadrupole modal-completeness theorem gives, for all retained quadrupole-active modes below `Omega`,

```math
\boxed{
\sum_n\kappa_{g,n}
\le
\frac{4G}{3c^5}I\Omega^4.
}
```

Therefore

```math
\boxed{
K_g\in\mathcal S_2
}
```

follows automatically in the retained band.

This is a useful structural closure:

```text
classical inertia sum rule
        -> finite total gravitational damping trace
        -> gravitational port operator is Hilbert–Schmidt
        -> infinite-dimensional passive H2 endpoint norm is finite
        -> same selected-port cut-set inequality.
```

The material theorem does not merely bound a modal sum; it supplies precisely the Schatten-class regularity needed by the infinite-dimensional transfer theorem.

## 7. End-to-end consequence

Let source and receiver satisfy the preceding Hilbert-space passive conditions, and let the direct one-pass propagation operator satisfy

```math
\sup_{\omega\in\mathcal B}
\|P_g(\omega)\|_{\rm op}^2
```

The same operator/Frobenius inequality used in the finite-dimensional proof gives

```math
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\le
\eta_{\max}
\min\!\left[
\|K_{g,A}\|_{\rm HS}^2,
\|K_{g,B}\|_{\rm HS}^2
\right].

```

Using the inertia resource at both endpoints reproduces

```math
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\le
\eta_{\max}\frac{4G\Omega^4}{3c^5}
\min(I_A,I_B).
}
```

The compact TT propagation ceiling then gives the same final narrowband theorem as before.

Therefore the phrase **"arbitrarily many passive resonances"** no longer needs to mean "every finite truncation." It is literal for any countable passive Markov modal sector satisfying the stated bounded-coupling realization.

## 8. What this does not prove

This extension is deliberately narrower than the full theory of arbitrary passive PDE boundary systems.

It does **not** claim to cover, without additional admissibility analysis,

- unbounded port operators;
- singular boundary control/observation maps;
- non-Markov memory kernels;
- arbitrary continuous spectral matter degrees of freedom;
- nonlinear or time-dependent dynamics;
- recurrent source–receiver gravitational multiple scattering.

Those are unnecessary for the present compact elastic normal-mode theorem.

A standard bounded-domain elastic body with a countable normal-mode basis and bounded band-local Markov port coupling lies inside the sufficient class above.

## 9. Relation to established infinite-dimensional systems theory

The mathematical ingredients are not claimed as new.

Relevant primary precedents include:

1. J. S. Baras and R. W. Brockett, **"H2-Functions and Infinite-Dimensional Realization Theory,"** SIAM Journal on Control 13, 221–241 (1975), DOI `10.1137/0313013`. This establishes the connection between strongly continuous semigroup realizations and square-integrable analytic transfer functions on a half-plane.

2. M. R. Opmeer, T. Reis, and W. Wollner, **"Finite-Rank ADI Iteration for Operator Lyapunov Equations,"** SIAM Journal on Control and Optimization (2013), DOI `10.1137/120885310`. This explicitly treats controllability Gramian operators for infinite-dimensional systems under semigroup/admissibility assumptions.

3. A. Hastir and B. Jacob, **"LQ optimal control for infinite-dimensional passive systems,"** arXiv:`2506.03882` (2025). This treats general infinite-dimensional passive systems and emphasizes contraction structure of the associated cost operator, including energy-preserving cases.

The contribution here is only the observation that the specific Experiment 02 endpoint proof lifts directly to the bounded-port Hilbert-space setting and that the gravitational inertia sum rule automatically supplies the Hilbert–Schmidt regularity needed for the transfer trace.
