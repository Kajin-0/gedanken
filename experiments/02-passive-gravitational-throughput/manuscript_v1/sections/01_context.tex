\section{Problem and result}
\label{sec:context}

Classical gravitational-antenna theory already contains most of the single-interface ingredients used below. Compact resonant antennas admit eigenmode descriptions of gravitational emission and reception, quadrupole-controlled effective areas, reciprocity, directivity, and quality-factor-independent short-pulse response \cite{HirakawaNariharaFujimoto1976,PaikWagoner1976}. Arbitrary-body resonant-mass theory supplies the long-wavelength STF tidal-force projection onto elastic modes \cite{Lobo1995}, while gravitational response sum rules have also been derived from susceptibility and quadrupole-commutator methods \cite{SrivastavaWidomPizzella2003}. Complete generator--receiver gravitational calculations are historical as well \cite{GrishchukSazhin1975,Rudenko2003}. None of those ingredients is claimed as new here.

The generic wave-system architecture is likewise established. Orthogonal source--receiver communication channels and sums of squared connection strengths are standard wave theory \cite{Miller2000}; passive two-body transfer bounds place separately constrained responses around a free-space Green operator \cite{MoleskyVenkataramJinRodriguez2020}; and continuous-time transducer metrics naturally integrate frequency-dependent channel quantities \cite{WangLiJiang2022}. The question addressed here is narrower:
\begin{quote}
Can the integrated coherent transfer of a separated compact passive gravitational link be bounded after eliminating the source and receiver matching details in favor of a cumulative matter resource at both endpoints?
\end{quote}

Let $T(\omega)$ be the transfer matrix from selected local input channels of the source to selected local output channels of the receiver, with all unobserved passive ports retained in the physical model. Define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\equiv
\frac{1}{2\pi}
\int_{\mathcal B}\dd\omega\,
\Tr[T^\dagger(\omega)T(\omega)].}
\label{eq:Gammacoh}
\end{equation}
For a scalar channel this is the area under the power-transmissivity spectrum; it has units of inverse time. It is not itself an information capacity.

The proof has three physical inputs. First, passive selected-port identities reduce the end-to-end spectral area to the smaller total gravitational coupling resource of the two endpoints. Second, modal completeness closes each compact endpoint resource by its internal mass inertia. Third, the compact TT radiation channel supplies the free-space propagation ceiling. For a narrow band centered at angular frequency $\omega$ the result is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\lesssim
\frac{25G\omega^2}{12c^3R^2}
\min(I_A,I_B),}
\label{eq:headline}
\end{equation}
where $I_A$ and $I_B$ are internal mass inertia moments about the endpoint centers of mass.

The result is classical within its stated linear-harmonic class. Its content is the disappearance of endpoint quality factors, passive mode count, internal coherent mixing, gravitational branching fractions, and compact orientation from the leading ceiling. Quantum theory is used only later as an equivalent normalization and for channel-specific corollaries.

The scope is deliberately narrow: weak linearized gravity, compact nonrelativistic mass-quadrupole coupling, separated wave-zone propagation, passive time-invariant endpoint dynamics, and finite or countably infinite bounded-port Markov modal sectors. Active gain or pumping, extended phased apertures, relays or external cavities, reactive near-field coupling, higher multipoles, relativistic/nonlinear matter, arbitrary unbounded PDE boundary ports, and genuinely non-Markov continua are outside the theorem.
