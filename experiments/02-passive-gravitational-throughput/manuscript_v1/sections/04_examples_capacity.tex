\section{Specializations and quantum corollaries}
\label{sec:examplescapacity}

\subsection{Exact two-resonator check}

For one source and one receiver resonance let
\begin{equation}
\kappa_A=\kappa_{\rm in}+\kappa_{g,A}+\kappa_{i,A},
\qquad
\kappa_B=\kappa_{g,B}+\kappa_{\rm out}+\kappa_{i,B}.
\end{equation}
With scalar propagation factor $\eta_{\rm prop}$, the detuning-dependent transmissivity is
\begin{equation}
\tau(\Omega)
=\frac{\eta_{\rm prop}\kappa_{\rm in}\kappa_{g,A}\kappa_{g,B}\kappa_{\rm out}}
{[\Omega^2+(\kappa_A/2)^2][\Omega^2+(\kappa_B/2)^2]}.
\label{eq:twopoleTau}
\end{equation}
Direct integration gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm EBP}
=\frac{4\eta_{\rm prop}\kappa_{\rm in}\kappa_{g,A}\kappa_{g,B}\kappa_{\rm out}}
{\kappa_A\kappa_B(\kappa_A+\kappa_B)}}
\label{eq:exactEBP}
\end{equation}
and passivity implies
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm EBP}\le
\eta_{\rm prop}\min(\kappa_{g,A},\kappa_{g,B}).}
\label{eq:twopolecut}
\end{equation}
Thus the general cut set is already visible in the minimal resonator pair. For equal gravitational linewidths, no internal loss, and $\kappa_{\rm in}=\kappa_{\rm out}=x\kappa_g$, the area is proportional to $2x^2/(1+x)^3$ and is maximized at
\begin{equation}
\boxed{x=2,\qquad
\Gamma_{\rm EBP}^{\rm sym,max}=\frac8{27}\eta_{\rm prop}\kappa_g.}
\label{eq:827}
\end{equation}
The optimum spectral area therefore differs from peak critical coupling. Appendix~\ref{app:twopole} gives the elementary proof.

\subsection{A compact mode of the correct scale}

For four equal endpoint masses $\mu$ at radius $L$ in a plus-quadrupole mode,
\begin{equation}
\kappa_g^{(4)}=\frac{8G\mu L^2\omega^4}{5c^5},
\label{eq:fourmasswidth}
\end{equation}
while $I=4\mu L^2$. Comparing with Eq.~\eqref{eq:materialbound} gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\frac{\kappa_g^{(4)}}{\kappa_{g,{\rm sum}}^{\max}}
=\frac3{10}.}
\label{eq:threetenths}
\end{equation}
The same plus tensor saturates the compact TT geometry bound. This demonstrates the correct scaling and order-unity proximity of an explicit mechanical mode, but does not establish simultaneous saturation of the complete theorem.

\subsection{Quantum-channel corollaries}

The throughput theorem is classical. After quantizing the same passive input/output fields, specialize to a stationary vacuum pure-loss realization. If $\tau_n(\omega)$ are the transmission eigenvalues, passivity gives
\begin{equation}
\tau_n(\omega)\le\|P_g(\omega)\|_{\op}^2\le\eta_{\max}.
\label{eq:taumax}
\end{equation}
Continuous-time capacities are frequency integrals of the corresponding per-mode capacities \cite{WangLiJiang2022}. For pure loss, if $\eta_{\max}\le1/2$, every eigenchannel is antidegradable and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{Q_1=0.}
\label{eq:Q1zero}
\end{equation}
With unlimited two-way classical assistance,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
Q_2\le
\frac{\Gamma_{\rm coh}}
{\ln2\,(1-\eta_{\max})}.}
\label{eq:Q2bound}
\end{equation}
These are channel-specific consequences of the classical transfer ceiling, not the origin of it. Thermal noise, active gain, and non-vacuum environments require a different capacity analysis.
