\section{Exact resonator specialization and quantum-information corollaries}
\label{sec:examplescapacity}

\subsection{Two resonators with explicit local ports}

The general classical throughput theorem is easiest to interpret in the minimal source--receiver resonator model. Let the source linewidth be
\begin{equation}
\kappa_A=\kappa_{\rm in}+\kappa_{g,A}+\kappa_{i,A}
\end{equation}
and the receiver linewidth
\begin{equation}
\kappa_B=\kappa_{g,B}+\kappa_{\rm out}+\kappa_{i,B},
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_{\rm in}$ and $\kappa_{\rm out}$ are the useful local couplings and $\kappa_{i,j}$ collect all other passive loss. For a scalar propagation factor $\eta_{\rm prop}$, the stationary transmissivity as a function of detuning $\Omega$ is
\begin{equation}
\tau(\Omega)
=
\frac{
\eta_{\rm prop}
\kappa_{\rm in}\kappa_{g,A}
\kappa_{g,B}\kappa_{\rm out}}
{[\Omega^2+(\kappa_A/2)^2]
 [\Omega^2+(\kappa_B/2)^2]}.
\label{eq:twopoleTau}
\end{equation}
Using
\begin{equation}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\frac{\dd\Omega}
{(\Omega^2+a^2)(\Omega^2+b^2)}
=
\frac{\pi}{ab(a+b)},
\end{equation}
one obtains the exact spectral area
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm EBP}
\equiv
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\tau(\Omega)\dd\Omega
=
\frac{4\eta_{\rm prop}
\kappa_{\rm in}\kappa_{g,A}
\kappa_{g,B}\kappa_{\rm out}}
{\kappa_A\kappa_B(\kappa_A+\kappa_B)}.}
\label{eq:exactEBP}
\end{equation}
Passivity gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm EBP}
\le
\eta_{\rm prop}
\min(\kappa_{g,A},\kappa_{g,B}).}
\label{eq:twopolecut}
\end{equation}
Thus reducing ordinary loss can raise peak conversion by increasing gravitational branching, but the integrated response remains tied to the smaller intrinsic gravitational interface rate.

There is also a useful distinction between optimizing peak conversion and optimizing spectral area. For symmetric gravitational linewidths $\kappa_{g,A}=\kappa_{g,B}=\kappa_g$, zero internal loss, and symmetric external coupling
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{\rm in}=\kappa_{\rm out}=x\kappa_g,
\end{equation}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactEBP} becomes
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Gamma_{\rm EBP}}{\eta_{\rm prop}\kappa_g}
=
\frac{2x^2}{(1+x)^3}.
\end{equation}
The maximum occurs at
\begin{equation}
\boxed{x=2,}
\end{equation}
not at critical coupling $x=1$, and gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm EBP}^{\rm sym,max}
=\frac8{27}\eta_{\rm prop}\kappa_g.}
\label{eq:827}
\end{equation}
Peak efficiency and integrated throughput are therefore different optimization targets even before the cumulative material bound is imposed.

\subsection{Comparison with an explicit compact plus mode}

It is useful to compare the classical modal-completeness ceiling with a concrete compact mode of the same scaling. Consider four endpoint masses $\mu$ at radius $L$ moving in the plus-quadrupole pattern. In the long-wavelength limit its leading gravitational linewidth is
\begin{equation}
\kappa_g^{(4)}
=\frac{8G\mu L^2\omega^4}{5c^5},
\label{eq:fourmasswidth}
\end{equation}
while the endpoint inertia about the center is $I=4\mu L^2$. Equation~\eqref{eq:materialbound} gives the cumulative modal ceiling
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{g,{\rm sum}}^{\max}
=\frac{16G\mu L^2\omega^4}{3c^5},
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\frac{\kappa_g^{(4)}}
{\kappa_{g,{\rm sum}}^{\max}}
=\frac3{10}.}
\label{eq:threetenths}
\end{equation}
The same plus tensor lies entirely in the line-of-sight TT subspace and saturates the compact-quadrupole directivity ceiling. Thus an explicit conserved mechanical realization attains the correct material scaling within an order-unity factor while saturating the geometry bound. Equation~\eqref{eq:combined2} is nevertheless an upper bound; simultaneous saturation of every inequality is not claimed.

As an aggressive numerical scale, take $\kappa_g\simeq6.87\times10^{-26}\,{\rm s}^{-1}$ and $kR=10$, for which the maximal compact-quadrupole leading propagation factor is $\eta_{\rm prop}=25/1600=0.015625$. Then
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\rm prop}\kappa_g
\simeq1.07\times10^{-27}\,{\rm s}^{-1},
\end{equation}
while the symmetric two-pole area optimum is
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\rm EBP}^{\rm sym,max}
\simeq3.18\times10^{-28}\,{\rm s}^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The inverse of the latter is of order $10^{20}$ years. This is an inverse coherent-transfer scale, not a literal ``time per qubit.''

\subsection{Quantum-channel corollaries}

The throughput theorem above is classical. To obtain quantum-information consequences, quantize the same passive input/output fields and specialize to a stationary vacuum realization in which all unobserved channels are pure loss. Let $\tau_n(\omega)$ be the transmission eigenvalues of $T^\dagger T$. Continuous-time bosonic transducer capacities are obtained by integrating the appropriate per-frequency channel quantity \cite{WangLiJiang2022}. Passive source and receiver subblocks are contractions, so
\begin{equation}
\tau_n(\omega)
\le
\|P_g(\omega)\|_{\op}^2
\le\eta_{\max}.
\label{eq:taumax}
\end{equation}
For a pure-loss bosonic channel, the unassisted quantum capacity per mode is
\begin{equation}
q_1(\tau)
=\max\!\left\{
\log_2\frac{\tau}{1-\tau},0
\right\}.
\end{equation}
Hence if
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\max}\le\frac12,
\end{equation}
then every eigenchannel lies in the zero-capacity region and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{Q_1=0.}
\label{eq:Q1zero}
\end{equation}
For a compact quadrupole link deep in the wave zone, Eq.~\eqref{eq:TTpropbound} automatically places $\eta_{\max}$ far below $1/2$.

With unlimited two-way classical assistance, the pure-loss per-mode capacity is
\begin{equation}
q_2(\tau)=-\log_2(1-\tau).
\end{equation}
For $0\le\tau\le\eta_{\max}<1$,
\begin{equation}
-\ln(1-\tau)
\le
\frac{\tau}{1-\eta_{\max}},
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
Q_2
\le
\frac{\Gamma_{\rm coh}}
{\ln2\,(1-\eta_{\max})}.}
\label{eq:Q2bound}
\end{equation}
Combining Eq.~\eqref{eq:Q2bound} with Eq.~\eqref{eq:combined2} yields a two-way entanglement-distribution-rate ceiling for the stationary vacuum pure-loss realization. The statement is deliberately channel specific: thermal occupation, dephasing, active gain, or pump noise changes the capacity problem even though the classical passive throughput theorem remains the physical starting bound.
