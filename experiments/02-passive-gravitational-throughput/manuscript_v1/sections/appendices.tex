\section{Passive selected-port Gramian bound}
\label{app:gramian}

For the passive endpoint of Eq.~\eqref{eq:passiveio}, define the complete controllability Gramian
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm all}
=\int_0^\infty\dd t\,
\ee^{At}K^\dagger K\ee^{A^\dagger t}.
\end{equation}
It solves
\begin{equation}
AP_{\rm all}+P_{\rm all}A^\dagger+K^\dagger K=0.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:Apassive} also gives
\begin{equation}
AI+IA^\dagger+K^\dagger K=0.
\end{equation}
For stable $A$, the Lyapunov solution is unique, hence
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm all}=I.
\end{equation}
For a selected input subset $u$,
\begin{equation}
P_u=\int_0^\infty\dd t\,
\ee^{At}K_u^\dagger K_u\ee^{A^\dagger t}.
\end{equation}
Since $0\le K_u^\dagger K_u\le K^\dagger K$, the integral representation gives
\begin{equation}
0\le P_u\le I.
\end{equation}
The standard Gramian identity then yields
\begin{equation}
\|S_{g\leftarrow u}\|_2^2
=\Tr(K_gP_uK_g^\dagger)
\le\Tr(K_g^\dagger K_g).
\end{equation}
The receiver-side statement follows analogously from the corresponding observability or dual controllability Gramian. No commutator or quantum-state assumption enters this proof.

\section{Classical quadrupole modal-completeness bound}
\label{app:classicalsum}

For the mass-weighted displacement space of Eq.~\eqref{eq:massinner}, define the tensor-indexed vector fields of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gijfield},
\begin{equation}
(g^{ij})_k
=\delta_{ik}x_j+\delta_{jk}x_i
-\frac23\delta_{ij}x_k.
\end{equation}
Their pointwise squared norm is
\begin{align}
\sum_{ijk}|(g^{ij})_k|^2
&=\sum_{ijk}
\left(
\delta_{ik}x_j+\delta_{jk}x_i
-\frac23\delta_{ij}x_k
\right)^2\\
&=8r^2-\frac{8}{3}r^2+\frac{4}{3}r^2\\
&=\frac{20}{3}r^2.
\end{align}
Here the first term is the squared norm of the symmetric part $\delta_{ik}x_j+\delta_{jk}x_i$, including its cross term; the last two terms arise from the trace subtraction and its interference with the symmetric part.

Let $\bm e_n=\bm w_n/\sqrt{\mu_n}$ be any orthonormal retained set of elastic modes. For each pair $i,j$, Bessel's inequality gives
\begin{equation}
\sum_n
|\langle\bm e_n,\bm g^{ij}\rangle_\rho|^2
\le
\|\bm g^{ij}\|_\rho^2.
\end{equation}
Since
\begin{equation}
\langle\bm e_n,\bm g^{ij}\rangle_\rho
=\frac{q_{n,ij}}{\sqrt{\mu_n}},
\end{equation}
summing over $i,j$ yields
\begin{align}
\sum_n\frac{q_n:q_n}{\mu_n}
&\le
\int\rho
\sum_{ijk}|(g^{ij})_k|^2dV\\
&=\frac{20}{3}\int\rho r^2dV\\
&=\boxed{\frac{20}{3}I}.
\end{align}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:HirakawaAG},
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\sum_n M A_{Gn}
\le\frac{40}{3}I.}
\end{equation}
This is the classical oscillator-strength sum rule used in the main proof.

If the elastic eigenmodes form a complete basis for the components of all five STF quadrupole-gradient fields, Bessel's inequality becomes Parseval equality. For a selected band, constrained mechanical subspace, or any incomplete retained set, the inequality is the appropriate form. Choosing the origin at the center of mass removes overlap with rigid translations. Infinitesimal rigid rotations of an anisotropic body generally rotate its static quadrupole and need not be orthogonal to the quadrupole-gradient fields. If free-body rotational zero modes are omitted from the retained positive-frequency elastic sector, Bessel's inequality only becomes looser; if rotational motion is constrained into a finite-frequency retained mode, it is included in the modal sum in the usual way.

\section{Equivalent quantum cumulative spectral bound}
\label{app:sumrule}

For a passive diagonal quantum state
\begin{equation}
\rho=\sum_m p_m|m\rangle\langle m|,
\end{equation}
with $p_m\ge p_n$ for $E_m<E_n$, the double-commutator identity for a Hermitian operator $F$ is
\begin{equation}
\frac12\Tr\rho[F,[H,F]]
=\sum_{m<n}(p_m-p_n)(E_n-E_m)|F_{mn}|^2.
\end{equation}
For the five orthonormal STF mass-quadrupole components $Q_a$ of ordinary nonrelativistic matter,
\begin{equation}
\sum_a\frac12\Tr\rho[Q_a,[H,Q_a]]
=\frac{10}{3}\hbar^2\langle I\rangle_\rho.
\end{equation}
Define the positive spectral measure
\begin{equation}
\dd\mu_Q(\omega)
=\sum_{m<n,a}
(p_m-p_n)|Q^a_{mn}|^2
\delta(\omega-\omega_{nm})\dd\omega.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\int_0^\infty\omega\,\dd\mu_Q(\omega)
=\frac{10}{3}\hbar\langle I\rangle_\rho.
\end{equation}
The positive gravitational transition-rate weight below $\Omega$ is
\begin{equation}
K_g(\Omega)
=\frac{2G}{5\hbar c^5}
\int_0^\Omega\omega^5\dd\mu_Q(\omega).
\end{equation}
Since $\omega^5\le\Omega^4\omega$ on the selected interval,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
K_g(\Omega)
\le
\frac{4G}{3c^5}
\langle I\rangle_\rho\Omega^4.}
\end{equation}
For a ground-state linear-harmonic system this reproduces the classical modal-completeness result after quantizing the normal coordinates. At finite temperature the population-difference spectral measure controls net quantum absorption and noise; it should not be confused with a reduction of the underlying classical mode-coupling constants.

\section{Independent aligned-plus TT integral}
\label{app:TTcheck}

Take $Q=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,0)$ and propagation along $z$. With the usual spherical transverse basis and unit-normalized polarization tensors,
\begin{align}
Q:\epsilon_+
&=\frac{1+\cos^2\theta}{\sqrt2}\cos2\phi,\\
Q:\epsilon_\times
&=-\sqrt2\cos\theta\sin2\phi.
\end{align}
For the normalized angular mode in Eq.~\eqref{eq:normalizedmode}, azimuthal integration gives
\begin{equation}
\sum_\lambda\int_0^{2\pi}\dd\phi\,
|u_{Q,\lambda}|^2
=\frac5{32}(1+6x^2+x^4),
\qquad x=\cos\theta.
\end{equation}
Thus the translated overlap is
\begin{equation}
S(z)
=\frac5{32}\int_{-1}^{1}
(1+6x^2+x^4)\ee^{izx}\dd x.
\end{equation}
The endpoint weight is $5/4$ at both $x=\pm1$. A single integration by parts therefore gives the outgoing contribution
\begin{equation}
S_+(z)
=-\frac{5i}{4z}\ee^{iz}+O(z^{-2}).
\end{equation}
Evaluating the polynomial Fourier integral exactly gives
\begin{equation}
S_+(z)
=-\frac{5i}{4}\frac{P(z)\ee^{iz}}{z^5},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
P(z)=3-3iz-3z^2+2iz^3+z^4.
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
|S_+(z)|^2
=\frac{25}{16z^2}
\left(
1-\frac2{z^2}+\frac3{z^4}
-\frac9{z^6}+\frac9{z^8}
\right).
\end{equation}
This check fixes the leading $25/16$ coefficient directly from normalized TT angular modes.

\section{Exact two-pole integral and symmetric optimum}
\label{app:twopole}

Substitution of $a=\kappa_A/2$ and $b=\kappa_B/2$ into
\begin{equation}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\dd\Omega}
{(\Omega^2+a^2)(\Omega^2+b^2)}
=\frac{\pi}{ab(a+b)}
\end{equation}
gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactEBP} immediately. To prove Eq.~\eqref{eq:twopolecut}, note first that
\begin{equation}
4\kappa_{g,B}\kappa_{\rm out}
\le(\kappa_{g,B}+\kappa_{\rm out})^2
\le\kappa_B^2
\end{equation}
and $\kappa_{\rm in}\le\kappa_A$. Hence
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\rm EBP}
\le
\eta_{\rm prop}\kappa_{g,A}
\frac{\kappa_B}{\kappa_A+\kappa_B}
\le
\eta_{\rm prop}\kappa_{g,A}.
\end{equation}
Exchanging $A$ and $B$ gives the receiver-side bound.

For the symmetric lossless family, define
\begin{equation}
f(x)=\frac{2x^2}{(1+x)^3}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
f'(x)=\frac{2x(2-x)}{(1+x)^4},
\end{equation}
so the unique positive maximum occurs at $x=2$, with $f(2)=8/27$.
