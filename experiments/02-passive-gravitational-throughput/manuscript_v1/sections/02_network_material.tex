\section{Passive-network cut set and material resource}
\label{sec:networkmaterial}

\subsection{Passive linear endpoint}

Consider a finite-dimensional bosonic endpoint with internal mode vector $\bm a$ and a stable completely passive input--output realization
\begin{equation}
\dot{\bm a}=A\bm a-K^\dagger\bm b_{\rm in},
\qquad
\bm b_{\rm out}=\bm b_{\rm in}+K\bm a,
\label{eq:passiveio}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A=-iH-\frac12K^\dagger K,
\qquad H=H^\dagger.}
\label{eq:Apassive}
\end{equation}
This is standard passive quantum input--output theory \cite{GutaYamamoto2014}. We partition the external channels into useful local ports $u$, gravitational ports $g$, and all remaining passive loss ports $\ell$,
\begin{equation}
K=\begin{pmatrix}K_u\\K_g\\K_\ell\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The local-input to gravitational-output transfer block is
\begin{equation}
S_{g\leftarrow u}(s)
=-K_g(sI-A)^{-1}K_u^\dagger.
\label{eq:Sgu}
\end{equation}
For any strictly proper matrix transfer $S$, define
\begin{equation}
\|S\|_2^2
\equiv
\frac{1}{2\pi}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dd\omega\,
\Tr[S^\dagger(i\omega)S(i\omega)].
\label{eq:H2def}
\end{equation}

For asymptotically stable completely passive systems the full-channel controllability Gramian is the identity, an established result in linear quantum-system theory \cite{TechakesariNurdin2017}. The selected-input Gramian
\begin{equation}
P_u=\int_0^\infty \dd t\,
\ee^{At}K_u^\dagger K_u\ee^{A^\dagger t}
\end{equation}
therefore satisfies $0\le P_u\le I$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\|S_{g\leftarrow u}\|_2^2
=
\Tr(K_gP_uK_g^\dagger)
\le
\boxed{\Tr(K_g^\dagger K_g)}.
\label{eq:endpointbound}
\end{equation}
The same inequality holds for the receiver transfer from gravitational input to useful local output.

Let $P_g(\omega)$ denote the normalized one-way gravitational propagation operator from the source gravitational output space to the receiver gravitational input space, and let
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\max}
\equiv
\sup_{\omega\in\mathcal B}
\|P_g(\omega)\|_{\op}^2.
\label{eq:etamax}
\end{equation}
For
\begin{equation}
T(\omega)=
S_{v\leftarrow g}^{(B)}(i\omega)
P_g(\omega)
S_{g\leftarrow u}^{(A)}(i\omega),
\label{eq:fullT}
\end{equation}
passive scattering subblocks are contractions. Applying the Frobenius/operator-norm inequality on either side of the propagation cut gives the following result.

\begin{theorem}[Passive gravitational cut set]
For two stable finite-dimensional completely passive linear endpoints connected by a one-way contractive gravitational propagation operator,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\le
\eta_{\max}
\min\!\left[
\Tr(K_{g,A}^\dagger K_{g,A}),
\Tr(K_{g,B}^\dagger K_{g,B})
\right].}
\label{eq:cutset}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The theorem permits arbitrary coherent mode mixing and overlapping resonances inside each endpoint. The passive Gramian identities themselves are not new; their role here is to expose the gravitational coupling trace as an endpoint resource that an end-to-end link cannot exceed.

\subsection{Separating coupling magnitude from radiation geometry}

To connect Eq.~\eqref{eq:cutset} to microscopic gravity without double counting angular coupling, consider the narrowband matter-to-graviton coupling operator
\begin{equation}
G_A:\mathcal H_{m,A}\rightarrow\mathcal H_g.
\end{equation}
Its gravitational damping Gram operator is
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{g,A}=G_A^\dagger G_A.
\end{equation}
The polar decomposition gives
\begin{equation}
G_A=V_A\Gamma_{g,A}^{1/2},
\qquad
G_B=V_B\Gamma_{g,B}^{1/2},
\label{eq:polar}
\end{equation}
where $V_A$ and $V_B$ are partial isometries into the normalized gravitationally bright radiation subspaces. If $U_R$ denotes free fixed-frequency graviton propagation, then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
G_B^\dagger U_RG_A
=
\Gamma_{g,B}^{1/2}
P_g
\Gamma_{g,A}^{1/2},
\qquad
P_g=V_B^\dagger U_RV_A.}
\label{eq:microscopicfactor}
\end{equation}
Thus the endpoint linewidth resource and the normalized propagation geometry are distinct factors. Nonorthogonal radiation patterns are retained in the off-diagonal entries of $\Gamma_g$; no independent bath channel per matter mode is assumed.

\subsection{Mass-quadrupole spectral ceiling}

We now specialize the endpoint resource to compact nonrelativistic linear matter. Diagonalizing the isolated quadratic matter Hamiltonian into normal modes gives, by unitary invariance,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Tr(K_g^\dagger K_g)=\sum_n\kappa_{g,n}.}
\label{eq:tracewidths}
\end{equation}
For a one-quantum mass-quadrupole transition of angular frequency $\omega_n$, weak linearized gravity gives \cite{BoughnRothman2006,PalessandroSloth2020}
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{g,n}
=
\frac{2G\omega_n^5}{5\hbar c^5}
Q_{ij}^{0n}Q_{ij}^{n0}.
\label{eq:gravitonrate}
\end{equation}

The normalization of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gravitonrate} can be cross-checked directly against classical compact-antenna theory. Hirakawa, Narihara, and Fujimoto define for an elastic mode $\bm u=x_n\bm w_n$ a modal mass $\mu_n=\int\rho|\bm w_n|^2dV$, a linearized STF quadrupole coefficient $q_{n,ij}$, and a gravitational effective area \cite{HirakawaNariharaFujimoto1976}
\begin{equation}
A_{Gn}=\frac{2\,q_n:q_n}{M\mu_n}.
\label{eq:HirakawaAG}
\end{equation}
Quantizing the same coordinate gives
\begin{equation}
Q_{ij}^{01}
=q_{n,ij}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\mu_n\omega_n}},
\qquad
Q^{01}:Q^{10}
=\frac{\hbar M A_{Gn}}{4\omega_n}.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:gravitonrate} therefore becomes
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\kappa_{g,n}
=\frac{GMA_{Gn}\omega_n^4}{10c^5}.}
\label{eq:HirakawaKappa}
\end{equation}
This is the quantized form of the historical mode oscillator-strength normalization, rather than a separate convention. Hirakawa et al.'s quality-factor-independent short-pulse result may equivalently be written
\begin{equation}
\frac{E}{F(\nu_n)}
=\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\kappa_{g,n}}{k_n^2}f_n(\hat n),
\label{eq:HirakawaPulseKappa}
\end{equation}
where $F(\nu)$ is incident gravitational pulse energy per unit area per hertz. We use these relations only as an independent normalization check; the single-mode oscillator-strength result is historical prior physics.

Let $Q_a$, $a=1,\ldots,5$, be an orthonormal STF basis for the internal mass-quadrupole operator, defined about the endpoint center of mass. Energy-weighted coordinate-multipole sum rules may be written as double commutators \cite{LuJohnson2018,Hinohara2019}. For the present mass quadrupole,
\begin{equation}
\sum_a\frac12
\langle0|[Q_a,[H,Q_a]]|0\rangle
=
\frac{10}{3}\hbar^2\langle I\rangle,
\label{eq:EWSR}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
I=\sum_r m_r|\bm r_r-\bm R_{\rm CM}|^2
\label{eq:inertia}
\end{equation}
is the internal inertia moment. Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{n,a}\omega_n|Q^a_{0n}|^2
=
\frac{10}{3}\hbar\langle I\rangle.
\end{equation}
For all retained quadrupole-active modes with $0<\omega_n\le\Omega$, positivity and $\omega_n^5\le\Omega^4\omega_n$ yield
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Tr(K_g^\dagger K_g)
\le
\frac{4G}{3c^5}
\langle I\rangle\Omega^4.}
\label{eq:materialbound}
\end{equation}
This is a cumulative oscillator-strength bound: adding passive resonances redistributes a finite gravitational coupling resource rather than producing unlimited integrated response.

Combining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:cutset} and \eqref{eq:materialbound} gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\le
\eta_{\max}
\frac{4G\Omega^4}{3c^5}
\min\!\left(
\langle I_A\rangle,
\langle I_B\rangle
\right).}
\label{eq:materialcutset}
\end{equation}
The remaining task is to bound $\eta_{\max}$ for direct propagation between compact quadrupole radiation spaces.
