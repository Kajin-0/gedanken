\section{Passive-network cut set and cumulative material resource}
\label{sec:networkmaterial}

\subsection{Passive linear endpoint}

Consider first an energy-normalized finite modal sector with internal complex-envelope mode vector $\bm a$ and passive realization
\begin{equation}
\dot{\bm a}=A\bm a-K^\dagger\bm b_{\rm in},
\qquad
\bm b_{\rm out}=\bm b_{\rm in}+K\bm a,
\label{eq:passiveio}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
A=-iH-\frac12K^\dagger K,
\qquad H=H^\dagger.}
\label{eq:Apassive}
\end{equation}
The same energy-normalized form describes classical passive coupled modes and passive bosonic quantum input--output systems; we use the latter literature for the standard finite-dimensional realization and Gramian identities \cite{GutaYamamoto2014,TechakesariNurdin2017}. Partition the external channels into useful local ports $u$, gravitational ports $g$, and all remaining passive loss ports $\ell$,
\begin{equation}
K=\begin{pmatrix}K_u\\K_g\\K_\ell\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The local-input to gravitational-output transfer block is
\begin{equation}
S_{g\leftarrow u}(s)
=-K_g(sI-A)^{-1}K_u^\dagger.
\label{eq:Sgu}
\end{equation}
For any strictly proper transfer $S$, define
\begin{equation}
\|S\|_2^2
\equiv
\frac{1}{2\pi}
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dd\omega\,
\Tr[S^\dagger(i\omega)S(i\omega)],
\label{eq:H2def}
\end{equation}
where in an infinite-dimensional channel space the trace is the Hilbert--Schmidt norm squared whenever it exists.

For a finite stable completely passive realization the selected-input controllability Gramian
\begin{equation}
P_u=\int_0^\infty \dd t\,
\ee^{At}K_u^\dagger K_u\ee^{A^\dagger t}
\end{equation}
satisfies $0\le P_u\le I$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\|S_{g\leftarrow u}\|_2^2
=
\Tr(K_gP_uK_g^\dagger)
\le
\boxed{\Tr(K_g^\dagger K_g)}.
\label{eq:endpointbound}
\end{equation}
The same inequality holds for the receiver transfer from gravitational input to useful local output. The proof is a passive linear-systems identity; quantization is not required.

Finite internal dimension is not essential to Eq.~\eqref{eq:endpointbound}. Infinite-dimensional $H^2$ realizations and operator controllability Gramians are standard \cite{BarasBrockett1975,OpmeerReisWollner2013}. For the particular passive form needed here, let $\mathcal X$ be a separable modal Hilbert space, let $H$ be self-adjoint on its domain, let $K:\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y$ be bounded, and let
\begin{equation}
A=-iH-\frac12K^\dagger K
\end{equation}
generate the contraction semigroup $\mathcal T(t)=e^{At}$. For every finite $\tau$,
\begin{align}
P_u(\tau)
&=\int_0^\tau\dd t\,
\mathcal T(t)K_u^\dagger K_u\mathcal T^\dagger(t)\\
&\le
\int_0^\tau\dd t\,
\mathcal T(t)K^\dagger K\mathcal T^\dagger(t)\\
&=I-\mathcal T(\tau)\mathcal T^\dagger(\tau)
\le I.
\label{eq:infgramian}
\end{align}
The monotone strong limit therefore gives $0\le P_u\le I$ without requiring a finite number of modes. If $K_g$ is Hilbert--Schmidt, then the causal impulse response $-K_g\mathcal T(t)K_u^\dagger$ is square-integrable in Hilbert--Schmidt norm and operator-valued Plancherel gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:endpointbound} unchanged. The gravitational material theorem below supplies precisely this regularity, since Eq.~\eqref{eq:materialbound} implies $\Tr(K_g^\dagger K_g)<\infty$ in the retained band. Thus a countably infinite bounded-port Markov modal sector is covered directly rather than only through finite truncations. Unbounded boundary ports and genuinely non-Markov continua require separate admissibility analysis and are not claimed here.

Let $P_g(\omega)$ denote the normalized one-way gravitational propagation operator from the source gravitational output space to the receiver gravitational input space, and let
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\max}
\equiv
\sup_{\omega\in\mathcal B}
\|P_g(\omega)\|_{\op}^2.
\label{eq:etamax}
\end{equation}
For
\begin{equation}
T(\omega)=
S_{v\leftarrow g}^{(B)}(i\omega)
P_g(\omega)
S_{g\leftarrow u}^{(A)}(i\omega),
\label{eq:fullT}
\end{equation}
passive scattering subblocks are contractions. Applying the Hilbert--Schmidt/operator-norm inequality on either side of the propagation cut gives the following result.

\begin{theorem}[Passive gravitational cut set]
For two passive linear endpoints in the finite-dimensional class above or its countably infinite bounded-port Hilbert-space extension, connected by a one-way contractive gravitational propagation operator and with finite gravitational coupling traces,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\le
\eta_{\max}
\min\!\left[
\Tr(K_{g,A}^\dagger K_{g,A}),
\Tr(K_{g,B}^\dagger K_{g,B})
\right].}
\label{eq:cutset}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The theorem permits arbitrary coherent mode mixing and overlapping resonances inside each endpoint, including a countably infinite passive modal sector under the stated bounded-port conditions. The passive Gramian and infinite-dimensional $H^2$ machinery are established prior art; their role here is to expose the gravitational coupling trace as the endpoint resource that an end-to-end link cannot exceed.

\subsection{Separating coupling magnitude from radiation geometry}

To connect Eq.~\eqref{eq:cutset} to microscopic gravity without double counting angular coupling, consider the narrowband matter-to-radiation coupling operator
\begin{equation}
G_A:\mathcal H_{m,A}\rightarrow\mathcal H_g.
\end{equation}
Its gravitational damping Gram operator is
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{g,A}=G_A^\dagger G_A.
\end{equation}
The polar decomposition gives
\begin{equation}
G_A=V_A\Gamma_{g,A}^{1/2},
\qquad
G_B=V_B\Gamma_{g,B}^{1/2},
\label{eq:polar}
\end{equation}
where $V_A$ and $V_B$ are partial isometries into normalized gravitationally bright radiation subspaces. If $U_R$ denotes fixed-frequency free propagation, then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
G_B^\dagger U_RG_A
=
\Gamma_{g,B}^{1/2}
P_g
\Gamma_{g,A}^{1/2},
\qquad
P_g=V_B^\dagger U_RV_A.}
\label{eq:microscopicfactor}
\end{equation}
Thus endpoint coupling magnitude and normalized propagation geometry are distinct factors. Nonorthogonal radiation patterns remain encoded in the off-diagonal entries of $\Gamma_g$; no independent radiation channel per matter mode is assumed.

\subsection{Classical modal oscillator-strength sum rule}

We now specialize the endpoint resource to compact nonrelativistic linear matter. Let $\rho(\bm r)$ be the mass density, choose the origin at the center of mass, and use the mass-weighted displacement inner product
\begin{equation}
\langle\bm u,\bm v\rangle_\rho
=\int_V\rho\,\bm u\cdot\bm v\,dV.
\label{eq:massinner}
\end{equation}
Let $\bm w_n$ be mutually orthogonal elastic normal modes with
\begin{equation}
\langle\bm w_n,\bm w_m\rangle_\rho
=\mu_n\delta_{nm}.
\end{equation}
For a small displacement $\bm u$, the first-order STF quadrupole change is
\begin{equation}
\delta Q_{ij}[\bm u]
=\int\rho\left(
 u_i x_j+u_j x_i
-\frac23\delta_{ij}\bm u\cdot\bm x
\right)dV.
\label{eq:linearQmap}
\end{equation}
Define displacement fields
\begin{equation}
(g^{ij})_k
=\delta_{ik}x_j+\delta_{jk}x_i
-\frac23\delta_{ij}x_k,
\label{eq:gijfield}
\end{equation}
so that the dynamic quadrupole coefficient of mode $n$ is
\begin{equation}
q_{n,ij}
=\langle\bm w_n,\bm g^{ij}\rangle_\rho.
\label{eq:qmodal}
\end{equation}
A direct Cartesian contraction gives the pointwise identity
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\sum_{i,j,k}|(g^{ij})_k|^2
=\frac{20}{3}r^2.}
\label{eq:gidentity}
\end{equation}
The projection/completeness mechanism used next is standard modal-participation or equivalent-modal-mass bookkeeping in structural dynamics \cite{BamfordGaymanWada1971}. The gravity-specific specialization is the set of STF quadrupole influence fields in Eq.~\eqref{eq:gijfield} and their exact total norm in Eq.~\eqref{eq:gidentity}.

Applying Bessel's inequality to the normalized modes $\bm e_n=\bm w_n/\sqrt{\mu_n}$ and then summing over $i,j$ yields
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\sum_n\frac{q_n:q_n}{\mu_n}
\le
\frac{20}{3}I,}
\qquad
I=\int\rho r^2dV.
\label{eq:classicalqsum}
\end{equation}
Equality requires completeness of the retained displacement modes for the five quadrupole-gradient fields; any band truncation or constrained modal subspace preserves the upper-bound form.

Hirakawa, Narihara, and Fujimoto define the compact gravitational effective area of mode $n$ as \cite{HirakawaNariharaFujimoto1976}
\begin{equation}
A_{Gn}=\frac{2\,q_n:q_n}{M\mu_n}.
\label{eq:HirakawaAG}
\end{equation}
Therefore Eq.~\eqref{eq:classicalqsum} immediately gives the classical cumulative effective-area sum rule
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\sum_n M A_{Gn}
\le
\frac{40}{3}I.}
\label{eq:classicalAGsum}
\end{equation}
This result contains no Planck constant.

Hirakawa's radiated-power normalization for a harmonic mode is
\begin{equation}
P_n
=\frac{G}{5c^5}M A_{Gn}\omega_n^4\langle T_n\rangle,
\end{equation}
where $\langle T_n\rangle$ is the cycle-averaged kinetic energy. Since the total cycle-averaged harmonic energy is $E_n=2\langle T_n\rangle$, the gravitational energy-decay rate is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\kappa_{g,n}
=\frac{P_n}{E_n}
=\frac{GMA_{Gn}\omega_n^4}{10c^5}.}
\label{eq:HirakawaKappa}
\end{equation}
In a normal-mode basis the trace of the gravitational damping Gram matrix is therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Tr(K_g^\dagger K_g)=\sum_n\kappa_{g,n}.}
\label{eq:tracewidths}
\end{equation}
For retained quadrupole-active modes with $0<\omega_n\le\Omega$,
\begin{equation}
\Tr(K_g^\dagger K_g)
\le
\frac{G\Omega^4}{10c^5}
\sum_nMA_{Gn},
\end{equation}
and Eq.~\eqref{eq:classicalAGsum} gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Tr(K_g^\dagger K_g)
\le
\frac{4G}{3c^5}I\Omega^4.}
\label{eq:materialbound}
\end{equation}
This is the cumulative endpoint oscillator-strength ceiling. Adding passive resonances redistributes a finite gravitational resource rather than producing unlimited integrated response. For a countably infinite retained modal sector, Eq.~\eqref{eq:materialbound} also proves that $K_g$ is Hilbert--Schmidt and therefore closes the regularity condition used in the infinite-dimensional endpoint theorem above.

\subsection{Equivalent quantum representation}

Quantization is not needed for Eq.~\eqref{eq:materialbound}, but it provides an independent normalization check and the bridge to quantum-channel corollaries. For a one-quantum transition of angular frequency $\omega_n$, weak linearized gravity gives \cite{BoughnRothman2006,PalessandroSloth2020}
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{g,n}
=\frac{2G\omega_n^5}{5\hbar c^5}
Q_{ij}^{0n}Q_{ij}^{n0}.
\label{eq:gravitonrate}
\end{equation}
Quantizing the same classical normal coordinate gives
\begin{equation}
Q_{ij}^{01}
=q_{n,ij}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\mu_n\omega_n}},
\qquad
Q^{01}:Q^{10}
=\frac{\hbar M A_{Gn}}{4\omega_n},
\end{equation}
which converts Eq.~\eqref{eq:gravitonrate} exactly into Eq.~\eqref{eq:HirakawaKappa}. Hirakawa et al.'s historical quality-factor-independent short-pulse result may correspondingly be written
\begin{equation}
\frac{E}{F(\nu_n)}
=\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\kappa_{g,n}}{k_n^2}f_n(\hat n),
\label{eq:HirakawaPulseKappa}
\end{equation}
where $F(\nu)$ is incident gravitational pulse energy per unit area per hertz.

Gravitational material-response sum rules are themselves historical: Srivastava, Widom, and Pizzella used dispersion relations to constrain the integrated absorptive response and evaluated the resulting sum rule through equal-time mass-quadrupole commutators \cite{SrivastavaWidomPizzella2003}. The relation in Eq.~\eqref{eq:classicalAGsum} is a different statement: it is a quadrupole-weighted modal-participation bound on the total compact elastic-mode effective area, directly in terms of the inertia $I$.

The same modal-completeness coefficient appears in the quantum mass-quadrupole EWSR. Let $Q_a$, $a=1,\ldots,5$, be an orthonormal STF basis for the internal quadrupole operator. Standard energy-weighted sum-rule identities give \cite{LuJohnson2018,Hinohara2019}
\begin{equation}
\sum_a\frac12
\langle0|[Q_a,[H,Q_a]]|0\rangle
=\frac{10}{3}\hbar^2\langle I\rangle,
\label{eq:EWSR}
\end{equation}
or equivalently
\begin{equation}
\sum_{n,a}\omega_n|Q^a_{0n}|^2
=\frac{10}{3}\hbar\langle I\rangle.
\end{equation}
For the linear-harmonic class this is the quantized representation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:classicalqsum}; both routes reproduce Eq.~\eqref{eq:materialbound}. The EWSR remains useful for spectral-response generalizations, but the present endpoint ceiling is already classical.

Combining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:cutset} and \eqref{eq:materialbound} gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\le
\eta_{\max}
\frac{4G\Omega^4}{3c^5}
\min(I_A,I_B).}
\label{eq:materialcutset}
\end{equation}
The remaining task is to bound $\eta_{\max}$ for direct propagation between compact quadrupole radiation spaces.
