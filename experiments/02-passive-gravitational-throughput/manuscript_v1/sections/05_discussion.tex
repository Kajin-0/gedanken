\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The result should be read as a gravity-specific resource closure, not as a new general formalism. Compact gravitational antenna eigenmodes, effective area, emission--reception reciprocity, $Q$-independent integrated response, and $D=5/2$ directivity are historical \cite{HirakawaNariharaFujimoto1976,PaikWagoner1976}. Complete generator--receiver analyses are also historical \cite{GrishchukSazhin1975,Rudenko2003}, as are gravitational response sum rules \cite{SrivastavaWidomPizzella2003}. The modal-completeness machinery is standard structural dynamics, passive Gramian and infinite-dimensional realization theory are established \cite{GutaYamamoto2014,TechakesariNurdin2017,BarasBrockett1975,OpmeerReisWollner2013}, and generic wave theory already supplies singular source--receiver channels, two-body Green-operator bounds, and scattering-network composition \cite{Miller2000,MoleskyVenkataramJinRodriguez2020,Redheffer1962}.

The narrower contribution is that these established pieces close on a particularly simple physical resource for compact gravitational transduction. The passive cut set reduces arbitrary endpoint matching and coherent mode mixing to $\Tr(K_g^\dagger K_g)$. Modal completeness then removes the individual gravitational oscillator strengths in favor of inertia, while normalized TT propagation removes the detailed compact radiation pattern. The final bound therefore depends only on
\begin{equation}
G,\qquad c,\qquad \omega,\qquad R,\qquad I_A,\qquad I_B.
\end{equation}
Within the stated class, higher mechanical quality factor can improve peak branching but not the integrated ceiling; additional passive modes can broaden or redistribute the response but not create oscillator strength; coherent bright/dark mixing leaves the gravitational coupling trace invariant; and compact orientation cannot exceed the TT projector/directivity limit. Repeated passive returns between the same two endpoints are subleading at the retained wave-zone order.

The countably infinite modal statement is likewise specific. It covers separable bounded-port Markov realizations for which the gravitational coupling operator is Hilbert--Schmidt; Eq.~\eqref{eq:materialbound} supplies that property. It does not establish a theorem for arbitrary unbounded boundary-control operators or non-Markov material continua. Those systems require the corresponding admissibility or response theory rather than a silent extension of Eq.~\eqref{eq:passiveio}.

Several routes can evade the theorem by changing its physical premises. Active gain, inversion, or parametric/time-dependent driving adds an external resource. Extended phased apertures can exceed compact quadrupole directivity. Relays, mirrors, or engineered external cavities change the propagation network. Near-field exchange, higher multipoles, relativistic motion, nonlinear matter, and strongly self-gravitating systems lie outside the retained compact nonrelativistic quadrupole model. The theorem is therefore a no-go statement about a defined passive architecture class, not a universal bound on gravitational communication.

The result is not claimed globally sharp. The explicit plus-quadrupole example reaches $3/10$ of the endpoint material ceiling while saturating the leading compact TT geometry, but the cut-set, material, and propagation inequalities need not be simultaneously saturable. Determining the globally optimal passive architecture is a separate variational problem.

Finally, quantization does not strengthen the physical ceiling. It converts the same classical oscillator strength into a one-graviton linewidth and permits information-theoretic interpretation. The pure-loss capacity statements of Sec.~\ref{sec:examplescapacity} are therefore secondary corollaries; thermal occupation and other noise processes change those capacities without changing the underlying classical oscillator-strength accounting.

\section{Conclusion}

For a separated compact passive gravitational link we have shown
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\lesssim
\frac{25G\omega^2}{12c^3R^2}\min(I_A,I_B).}
\end{equation}
The proof is a short composition of a generic passive selected-port cut, a compact gravitational inertia bound on the total endpoint coupling, and normalized TT wave-zone propagation. The individual methods are established; the claimed result is their gravity-specific two-ended closure. Within its declared class, passive resonant engineering can redistribute where and how the weak gravitational coupling appears, but cannot increase the leading frequency-integrated resource beyond the inertia-controlled ceiling.
