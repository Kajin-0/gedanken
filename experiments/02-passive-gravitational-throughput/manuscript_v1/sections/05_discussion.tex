\section{Discussion and scope}
\label{sec:discussion}

The point of Eq.~\eqref{eq:combined2} is not that compact gravitational antennas possess reciprocal emission and reception, that resonant response has an efficiency--bandwidth tradeoff, or that quadrupole directivity can reach $5/2$. Hirakawa, Narihara, and Fujimoto already treated compact mechanical eigenmode emission and reception in one reciprocal framework, used the same quadrupole-derived mode resource at both interfaces, obtained a quality-factor-independent short-pulse response, and derived the real-STF quadrupole directivity functional used here in equivalent component form \cite{HirakawaNariharaFujimoto1976}. Resonant-mass antenna theory likewise contains frequency-integrated absorption physics \cite{PaikWagoner1976}, while complete generator--detector feasibility calculations are historical \cite{GrishchukSazhin1975}. Susceptibility-based gravitational absorption is established \cite{SrivastavaWidomPizzella2003}; passive Gramian structure is established \cite{GutaYamamoto2014,TechakesariNurdin2017}; and frequency-integrated transducer capacities are standard in modern quantum-transduction theory \cite{WangLiJiang2022}. More generally, wave physics already provides orthogonal source--receiver communication channels and sums of squared connection strengths \cite{Miller2000}, as well as two-body transfer bounds in which separately constrained material responses are connected by a free-space Green operator and analyzed through Frobenius norms or transmission singular values \cite{MoleskyVenkataramJinRodriguez2020}.

The candidate contribution is narrower: a frequency-integrated passive H2 cut set is closed for gravity by a cumulative compact-matter oscillator-strength sum rule at both endpoints and by normalized separated TT propagation, yielding the explicit inertia-controlled ceiling in Eq.~\eqref{eq:combined2}. The material step can be proved classically. Bessel completeness of the elastic modes gives
\begin{equation}
\sum_n M A_{Gn}\le\frac{40}{3}I,
\end{equation}
which converts directly to Eq.~\eqref{eq:materialbound}. Quantization and the quadrupole EWSR reproduce the same coefficient. The absence of $\hbar$ from the headline theorem is therefore structural: no quantum resource is needed to establish the physical throughput ceiling.

This closure makes several common optimization directions transparent. Increasing endpoint $Q$ can suppress ordinary loss and improve peak gravitational branching, but it cannot increase the cumulative endpoint gravitational oscillator strength. The $Q$-independence of a short-pulse gravitational antenna response is historical; the additional statement here is that arbitrary passive local-port matching and coherent multimode dynamics remain bounded by the same total endpoint resource. Adding passive resonances redistributes oscillator strength but cannot exceed the modal-completeness ceiling. Coherent internal mode mixing can create bright and dark combinations, but the trace of the gravitational damping Gram matrix is basis invariant. Finally, both the real-STF directivity functional and its $D=5/2$ maximum are historical antenna physics. The TT representation used here normalizes the separated propagation operator entering the cut set; neither singular-channel propagation as a generic wave concept nor the directivity formula itself is a novelty claim.

The classical status of the throughput theorem also sharpens the quantum scope. Quantization identifies the classical gravitational decay rate with a one-graviton spontaneous-emission linewidth and embeds the same passive transfer matrix into a bosonic channel. Only then do questions such as finite-use entanglement survival, antidegradability, and assisted quantum capacity arise. The pure-loss statements in Sec.~\ref{sec:examplescapacity} should therefore be read as operational quantum-information consequences of a classical passive transfer bound, not as the origin of that bound.

That distinction also clarifies what can evade the theorem. Active gain, inversion, or parametric/time-dependent driving leaves the passive time-invariant class. An extended phased aperture can achieve larger directivity by using spatial phase across its physical size and is not a compact quadrupole. Higher multipoles, relativistic sources, strongly self-gravitating systems, and materially nonlinear motion leave the retained compact nonrelativistic linear-harmonic approximation. Reactive near-field exchange is a different gravitational channel from the direct one-way wave-zone propagation considered here. Intermediate relays create additional interfaces and propagation segments and require a separate multi-hop treatment. Curved-background focusing likewise replaces the flat-space propagation operator used in Sec.~\ref{sec:ttcombined}.

The modal-completeness material theorem has a correspondingly specific scope. It assumes a positive mass density, a linear displacement Hilbert space, and an orthogonal passive normal-mode expansion. For a retained subset of modes the Bessel inequality remains valid, but genuinely nonlinear, relativistic, velocity-dependent, or non-mechanical microscopic degrees of freedom require a different response resource. The quantum EWSR and more general positive spectral measures remain useful routes for such extensions, but they are not prerequisites for the present theorem.

Thermal occupation does not alter the classical oscillator-strength ceiling itself, although it changes signal-to-noise performance and, after quantization, the channel noise and capacities. We therefore make no thermal quantum-capacity claim from Eq.~\eqref{eq:materialbound} alone. The capacity statements in Sec.~\ref{sec:examplescapacity} apply only to the stationary vacuum pure-loss realization.

The theorem is also not claimed globally sharp. The compact plus mode in Sec.~\ref{sec:examplescapacity} saturates the TT geometry ceiling and reaches the correct material scaling within the order-unity ratio in Eq.~\eqref{eq:threetenths}, but the cut-set, material, and propagation inequalities need not all be saturated by the same physical device. Establishing the globally optimal passive architecture is a separate variational problem.

\section{Conclusion}

We have bounded the frequency-integrated coherent transfer of a direct propagating gravitational link between compact passive linear matter systems. Established passive-system identities first reduce the end-to-end problem to the smaller gravitational coupling resource of the source and receiver. A classical mass-weighted modal-completeness identity then bounds each cumulative quadrupole oscillator-strength resource by the endpoint inertia, while normalized TT propagation bounds the largest compact quadrupole free-space channel. In the narrowband wave zone,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\lesssim
\frac{25G\omega^2}{12c^3R^2}
\min(I_A,I_B).}
\end{equation}
Within the declared passive class, increasing quality factor, adding passive resonances, or coherently mixing compact quadrupole modes cannot evade this integrated ceiling. The same endpoint coefficient is reproduced by quantizing the elastic modes and applying the mass-quadrupole EWSR, and a stationary vacuum pure-loss realization inherits explicit quantum-capacity corollaries.

The result should be read as a classical gravity-specific cumulative many-mode resource bound with quantum-information consequences, not as a new gravitational antenna reciprocity/directivity law, a new generic singular-channel formalism, a first source--receiver calculation, or a universal limit on gravitational communication. Architectures that improve substantially on the ceiling must change at least one of the physical assumptions that generate it.
