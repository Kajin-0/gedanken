\begin{abstract}
Classical gravitational-antenna theory already contains compact eigenmode descriptions of emission and reception, gravitational reciprocity, quadrupole-controlled oscillator strength, and quality-factor-independent short-pulse response; resonant-mass theory likewise establishes finite frequency-integrated absorption. Modern quantum-transducer theory treats efficiency and bandwidth jointly. We ask a narrower end-to-end question: for two separated compact passive matter interfaces connected by direct propagating gravity, what upper bound follows for the frequency-integrated local-port-to-local-port coherent transfer when both endpoint gravitational resources and the intervening free-space channel are bounded explicitly? For stable passive linear bosonic source and receiver networks, we define
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathcal B}\dd\omega\,
\Tr[T^\dagger(\omega)T(\omega)].
\end{equation}
Established passive-system identities give a cut-set bound in terms of the total gravitational coupling resources of the two endpoints. For compact nonrelativistic matter, the mass-quadrupole energy-weighted sum rule bounds each cumulative resource, while a normalized transverse-traceless one-graviton calculation bounds the compact quadrupole propagation singular value by $5/(4kR)$ in the wave zone. For a narrow band centered at angular frequency $\omega$, these results give
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\lesssim
\frac{25G\omega^2}{12c^3R^2}
\min\!\left(\langle I_A\rangle,\langle I_B\rangle\right),
}
\end{equation}
where $I_A$ and $I_B$ are the internal mass inertia moments about the endpoint centers of mass. The bound is independent of endpoint quality factor, the number of passive internal resonances, coherent mode mixing, and compact quadrupole orientation within the stated class. A two-resonator specialization yields an exact efficiency--bandwidth integral and shows that the area-optimal external coupling differs from peak-optimal critical coupling. For a stationary vacuum pure-loss realization, the response bound also gives continuous-time quantum-capacity corollaries. The claimed contribution is the gravity-specific cumulative closure that converts the two endpoint coupling traces into the inertia-controlled bound above, not gravitational emission/reception, integrated antenna response, generic singular-channel wave transfer, or source--receiver analysis by itself. The result is not a universal limit on gravitational communication or on active, extended-aperture, near-field, or relativistic architectures.
\end{abstract}
