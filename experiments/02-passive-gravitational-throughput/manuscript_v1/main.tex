\begin{abstract}
We derive a frequency-integrated upper bound on coherent transfer between two separated compact passive matter systems coupled through propagating linearized gravity.  For a narrowband complex-envelope transfer matrix $T(\nu)$, define
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\rm coh}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathcal B_\nu}\dd\nu\,\Tr[T^\dagger(\nu)T(\nu)].
\end{equation}
Passivity bounds this spectral area by the smaller total gravitational coupling trace of the two endpoints.  For compact nonrelativistic linear-harmonic matter, mass-weighted modal completeness gives a cumulative quadrupole resource bounded by the scalar second mass moment $I_2=\int\rho r^2\dd^3x$.  Normalized transverse-traceless quadrupole radiation has maximum directivity $5/2$, which gives the leading separated-wave-zone propagation ceiling $25/[16(k_0R)^2]$.  Combining these results yields
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\lesssim
\frac{25G\omega_0^2}{12c^3R^2}\min(I_{2,A},I_{2,B})
}
\end{equation}
for carrier frequency $\omega_0$, envelope bandwidth $B\ll\omega_0$, and finite or countably infinite bounded-port Markov modal sectors.  Passive internal mode mixing and repeated passive returns between the same two compact endpoints do not change the retained leading ceiling.  Integrated resonant-mass response, gravitational-antenna modal theory, material-response sum rules, generator--receiver calculations, and generic passive-wave bounds are historical ingredients.  The claimed contribution is only their gravity-specific cumulative two-ended inertia closure; no priority claim is made.
\end{abstract}
