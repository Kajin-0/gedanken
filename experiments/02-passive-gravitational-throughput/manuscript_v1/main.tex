\begin{abstract}
Classical gravitational-antenna theory already contains compact eigenmode descriptions of emission and reception, gravitational reciprocity, quadrupole-controlled oscillator strength, and quality-factor-independent short-pulse response. Generic wave theory likewise provides singular source--receiver channels and two-body transfer bounds. We ask a narrower question: for two separated compact passive matter interfaces connected by direct propagating gravity, what upper bound follows for the frequency-integrated local-port-to-local-port coherent transfer when the cumulative gravitational oscillator strengths of both endpoints are constrained by the available matter resource? For stable passive linear source and receiver networks, define
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathcal B}\dd\omega\,
\Tr[T^\dagger(\omega)T(\omega)].
\end{equation}
Established passive-system identities give a cut-set bound in terms of the total gravitational coupling resources of the two endpoints. For compact linear matter, a classical mass-weighted modal-completeness identity gives
\begin{equation}
\sum_n M A_{Gn}\le \frac{40}{3}I,
\end{equation}
where $A_{Gn}$ is the historical gravitational effective area of mode $n$ and $I$ is the internal mass inertia moment. This is equivalent, after quantization, to the mass-quadrupole energy-weighted sum rule. Combined with compact transverse-traceless wave-zone propagation, it yields for a narrow band centered at angular frequency $\omega$
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\lesssim
\frac{25G\omega^2}{12c^3R^2}
\min\!\left(\langle I_A\rangle,\langle I_B\rangle\right).
}
\end{equation}
The bound is independent of endpoint quality factor, the number of passive internal resonances, coherent mode mixing, and compact quadrupole orientation within the stated class. The physical throughput ceiling is classical; quantization reproduces the same oscillator-strength normalization and gives stationary pure-loss quantum-capacity corollaries. The claimed contribution is the gravity-specific cumulative two-ended inertia closure, not gravitational emission/reception, integrated antenna response, generic singular-channel wave transfer, or quantum mechanics as the origin of the ceiling. The result is not a universal limit on active, extended-aperture, near-field, relativistic, or nonlinear gravitational architectures.
\end{abstract}
