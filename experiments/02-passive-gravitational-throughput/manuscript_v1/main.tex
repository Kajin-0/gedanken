\begin{abstract}
We bound the frequency-integrated coherent transfer between two separated compact passive matter systems coupled through propagating linearized gravity. For the local-port transfer matrix $T(\omega)$ define
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathcal B}\dd\omega\,
\Tr[T^\dagger(\omega)T(\omega)].
\end{equation}
A passive selected-port cut bounds this spectral area by the smaller total gravitational coupling resource of the two endpoints. For compact nonrelativistic linear-harmonic matter, standard modal completeness applied to the historical quadrupolar tidal coupling gives
\begin{equation}
\sum_n M A_{Gn}\le \frac{40}{3}I,
\end{equation}
where $A_{Gn}$ is the gravitational effective area of mode $n$ and $I$ is the internal mass inertia. This implies
\begin{equation}
\Tr(K_g^\dagger K_g)\le \frac{4G}{3c^5}I\omega^4
\end{equation}
in a narrow operating band. The normalized compact transverse-traceless channel obeys
\begin{equation}
\|P_g\|_{\op}^2\le \frac{25}{16(kR)^2}
\end{equation}
at leading wave-zone order. Combining the endpoint and propagation resources yields
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\lesssim
\frac{25G\omega^2}{12c^3R^2}
\min(I_A,I_B).
}
\end{equation}
The result is classical and is independent of endpoint quality factor, passive resonance count, coherent internal mode mixing, and compact quadrupole orientation. Countably infinite bounded-port Markov modal sectors obey the same endpoint cut, and passive reciprocal returns between the same two endpoints do not change the leading $1/R^2$ coefficient. Quantization reproduces the oscillator-strength normalization and gives secondary pure-loss channel corollaries. The claimed contribution is the gravity-specific two-ended inertia closure, not the established antenna, modal, passive-system, or wave-channel ingredients used to obtain it.
\end{abstract}
