\section{Interpretation, scale, and limitations}
\label{sec:interpretation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:finitegeometry} changes the interpretation of the endpoint resource. The leading far-zone ceiling is not controlled by the total scalar second moment alone. Only the $|m|=2$ quadrupole sector survives at order $R^{-2}$, and its complete modal oscillator strength is bounded by $4I_{\hat R}$. Geometry therefore enters the leading theorem through the ordinary moment of inertia about the line joining the endpoints.

The minimum over endpoints remains physically important. Either endpoint is a cut: increasing the available source resource cannot compensate for a receiver with a smaller allowed sector resource, and vice versa. Passive quality factor, modal count, and internal hybridization can redistribute response in frequency but do not create new sector oscillator strength.

\subsection{Scale and a resonant-bar check}

For a uniform sphere of mass $M$ and radius $a$,
\begin{equation}
I_{\hat R}=\frac25Ma^2,\qquad
Z_{\hat R}=\frac15Ma^2,
\end{equation}
for every direction. The leading narrowband far-zone result becomes
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\lesssim
\frac{G\omega_0^2Ma^2}{2c^3R^2}.
}
\label{eq:spherebound}
\end{equation}
Take $M=10^3\,\mathrm{kg}$, $a=1\,\mathrm{m}$, and $f_0=\omega_0/(2\pi)=1\,\mathrm{kHz}$. The compactness parameter is $\omega_0a/c\simeq2.1\times10^{-5}$. Choosing $k_0R=100$ gives $R\simeq4.77\times10^6\,\mathrm{m}$ and the leading result
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\rm coh}\lesssim2.15\times10^{-39}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Using the exact sector expression in Eq.~\eqref{eq:finitegeometry} at the same single frequency gives a factor $1.00020009$ relative to this leading value. Thus the finite-$kR$ outgoing compact-TT correction for the spherical example is about $0.020\%$ in the opposite direction from the isolated $\eta_2$ correction because the subleading $|m|=1$ and $m=0$ sectors contribute positively. Finite-source multipole corrections remain separately controlled by compactness.

A simple bar calculation tests the leading directional resource. Consider an ideal slender free--free uniform bar of length $L$ and mass $M$, neglecting transverse dimensions, with fundamental longitudinal displacement $w_x(x)=\sin(\pi x/L)$ for $-L/2\le x\le L/2$. For propagation perpendicular to the bar axis,
\begin{equation}
\mu=\frac{M}{2},\qquad
I_{\hat R}=\frac{ML^2}{12},
\end{equation}
and direct evaluation gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{q:q}{\mu}=\frac{64ML^2}{3\pi^4}.
\end{equation}
The bar quadrupole has $F_q/(q:q)=3/4$ in its maximum-radiation direction, so the same single mode occupies
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\frac{F_q/\mu}{4I_{\hat R}}
=\frac{48}{\pi^4}
\simeq0.493
}
\label{eq:barfraction}
\end{equation}
of the complete leading $|m|=2$ endpoint resource. This is a factor-of-two-level gap, not an orders-of-magnitude looseness generated by the completeness step. It still does not imply that a physical two-ended device approaches the full $\Gamma_{\rm coh}$ ceiling: local-port accessibility, nongravitational loss, matching, the second endpoint, and the retained-sector approximation can all reduce the realized transfer.

The sphere number is not a waiting time for an event or a bit. It is a spectral area. The simultaneous compactness and far-zone assumptions place ordinary macroscopic mechanical examples in an extraordinarily weak gravitational-coupling regime. The theorem is therefore primarily structural: it identifies a resource that passive resonance engineering cannot exceed within the stated class.

\subsection{Relation to prior work and scope}

The ingredients have substantial precedents. Resonant-mass absorption cross sections and integrated response are historical \cite{PaikWagoner1976,Aguiar2011}; arbitrary-body multimode gravitational response is established \cite{Lobo1995}; material-response dispersion and sum-rule methods have been developed \cite{Srivastava2003}; and complete gravitational generator--receiver analyses exist \cite{Rudenko2003}. In particular, broad-frequency material sum rules can close integrated response in terms of constitutive high- and low-frequency elastic quantities \cite{Srivastava2003}; that is a different resource from the mass-moment closure derived here. Generic wave-channel decompositions, passive $H_2$ methods, and multiple-scattering composition are likewise established \cite{Miller2000,Molesky2020,BarasBrockett1975,Opmeer2013,Redheffer1962}.

The closest classical engineering analogy is gain--bandwidth theory, but the correspondence is not identity. Fano's broadband-matching limits constrain reflection through logarithmic integral relations \cite{Fano1950}; Chu and Harrington constrain antenna size, stored energy, directivity, gain, and bandwidth \cite{Chu1948,Harrington1960}. Those results establish that passive matching and superdirectivity cannot evade generic bandwidth and size tradeoffs. The passive selected-port cut in Sec.~\ref{sec:passive} belongs to that broad family and is not the gravity-specific contribution. The present closure instead combines a gravitational quadrupole oscillator-strength relation, its STF-sector decomposition relative to the source--receiver axis, and compact TT propagation at both endpoints.

Recent gravity-communication work addresses different performance criteria and often different interaction regimes: Newtonian communication/noise constraints \cite{KafriMilburnTaylor2015}, LOCC simulation bounds \cite{LamiPedernalesPlenio2024}, state-transfer benchmarks for gravitationally interacting oscillators \cite{ToccaceloAndersenBrask2025}, and an optomechanical gravity-induced thermal-attenuator channel \cite{MariZippilliVitali2026}. These works motivate gravity as a mediator, but their information-theoretic criteria are not used here. $\Gamma_{\rm coh}$ remains a classical coherent-transfer spectral area.

The retained-sector restriction is consequential but should be interpreted precisely. Equation~\eqref{eq:kappan} is an on-shell linewidth. It is not a license to assign a far-detuned high-frequency mode its resonance-frequency $\omega_n^4$ coupling unchanged when evaluating a low-frequency tail. Such a mode lies outside the near-resonant Markov reduction and requires the full frequency-dependent elastodynamic and radiative response. At the same time, the unweighted completeness relation alone cannot produce an all-frequency $I_2$ bound on the formal Markov trace, because it does not control the fourth spectral moment. A genuinely whole-body inertia theorem would therefore require additional material regularity or a different frequency-domain argument.

The retained-sector assumption is needed only for the inertia-controlled strengthening. Even without it, a finite-band passive compact-quadrupole link cannot have unbounded transfer: at each frequency $\operatorname{rank}P_g\le5$ and the endpoint scattering blocks are contractions, so
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\le
\frac{5}{2\pi}\int_{\mathcal B_\nu}
\|P_g[\omega(\nu),R]\|_{\op}^2\,\dd\nu .
\label{eq:genericbandbound}
\end{equation}
This generic bound is usually far looser and contains no material resource; it only shows that uncontrolled internal modes do not create a mathematical divergence in a finite measured band.

The theorem deliberately excludes active gain, inversion, parametric pumping, externally powered feedback, extended phased apertures, added gravitational relays or external cavities, higher-multipole-dominated operation, relativistic or nonlinear matter, arbitrary unbounded PDE boundary ports without admissibility analysis, genuinely non-Markov continua, curved-background focusing, and reactive near-field exchange. These are changes of physical class, not corrections to a passive compact-TT theorem.

\section{Conclusion}

For a separated compact passive gravitational link, the strongest closure is obtained by preserving angular information rather than multiplying a total endpoint quadrupole sum by a worst-case propagation directivity. Decomposing STF quadrupole space into $m=0$, $|m|=1$, and $|m|=2$ sectors gives exact sectorwise completeness resources. Combined with the finite-$kR$ outgoing TT singular values, this yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:finitegeometry}, a finite-band propagation bound within the retained compact model that does not freeze the propagator at the carrier.

In the far zone the $|m|=1$ and $m=0$ sectors fall as $R^{-4}$ and $R^{-6}$ in power, leaving only the $|m|=2$ resource. The resulting leading narrowband ceiling is
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\lesssim
\frac{5G\omega_0^2}{4c^3R^2}
\min(I_{\hat R,A},I_{\hat R,B}).
\end{equation}
Thus the surviving endpoint quantity is the moment of inertia about the separation axis. Passive resonance count, passive $Q$, internal unitary mode mixing, and same-two-endpoint recurrence can redistribute response but cannot manufacture additional leading two-ended spectral area within the declared retained modal class.
