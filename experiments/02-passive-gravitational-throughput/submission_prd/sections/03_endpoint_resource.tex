\section{Sector-resolved gravitational endpoint resource}
\label{sec:resource}

Let a compact elastic body have equilibrium density $\rho(\bm x)$ and center of mass at the origin. Expand a small displacement field as
\begin{equation}
\bm u(\bm x,t)=\sum_n \xi_n(t)\bm w_n(\bm x),
\end{equation}
with mass inner product
\begin{equation}
\langle u,v\rangle_\rho=\int_V\rho\,\bm u\cdot\bm v\,\dd^3x,
\qquad
\langle w_n,w_m\rangle_\rho=\mu_n\delta_{nm}.
\end{equation}
The long-wavelength tidal coupling and elastic-mode treatment are standard gravitational-antenna theory \cite{Hirakawa1976,Lobo1995}.

To first order in displacement, define the STF modal quadrupole
\begin{equation}
q_{n,ij}
=\int_V\rho\left(
w_{n,i}x_j+w_{n,j}x_i
-\frac23\delta_{ij}\bm w_n\cdot\bm x
\right)\dd^3x .
\label{eq:qn}
\end{equation}
For a real normal mode with $\xi_n(t)=\Re[\xi_{0,n}e^{-i\omega_nt}]$, the standard quadrupole power and harmonic-mode energy are
\begin{align}
\overline P_{g,n}
&=\frac{G\omega_n^6}{10c^5}|\xi_{0,n}|^2(q_n:q_n),\\
E_n&=\frac12\mu_n\omega_n^2|\xi_{0,n}|^2,
\end{align}
so the on-shell gravitational energy-decay rate is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\kappa_{g,n}
=\frac{G\omega_n^4}{5c^5}
\frac{q_n:q_n}{\mu_n}.
}
\label{eq:kappan}
\end{equation}

The scalar completeness relation follows from the vector influence fields
\begin{equation}
(g^{ij})_k
=\delta_{ik}x_j+\delta_{jk}x_i
-\frac23\delta_{ij}x_k,
\end{equation}
for which $q_{n,ij}=\langle w_n,g^{ij}\rangle_\rho$ and
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i,j,k}|(g^{ij})_k|^2=\frac{20}{3}r^2 .
\end{equation}
For $\phi_n=w_n/\sqrt{\mu_n}$, Bessel's inequality therefore gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\sum_n\frac{q_n:q_n}{\mu_n}
\le\frac{20}{3}I_2 .
}
\label{eq:qsum}
\end{equation}
For a complete displacement basis this unweighted projection sum is a Parseval equality.

The propagation problem contains more structure than Eq.~\eqref{eq:qsum} uses. Choose an orthonormal triad $(\bm e_1,\bm e_2,\hat R)$ and an orthonormal real STF tensor basis adapted to rotations about $\hat R$:
\begin{align}
E_{2,+}&=\frac{\bm e_1\bm e_1-\bm e_2\bm e_2}{\sqrt2},
&
E_{2,\times}&=\frac{\bm e_1\bm e_2+\bm e_2\bm e_1}{\sqrt2},\\
E_{1,1}&=\frac{\bm e_1\hat R+\hat R\bm e_1}{\sqrt2},
&
E_{1,2}&=\frac{\bm e_2\hat R+\hat R\bm e_2}{\sqrt2},\\
E_0&=\frac{2\hat R\hat R-\bm e_1\bm e_1-\bm e_2\bm e_2}{\sqrt6}.
\end{align}
These span the $|m|=2$, $|m|=1$, and $m=0$ sectors respectively. Because every $E$ is traceless and symmetric,
\begin{equation}
q_n:E=\left\langle w_n,\,2E\bm x\right\rangle_\rho .
\label{eq:Einfluence}
\end{equation}
Applying Bessel separately to these five influence fields gives sector-resolved oscillator-strength bounds. Writing
\begin{equation}
Q_{m,n}^2=\|\Pi_m q_n\|^2
\end{equation}
for the squared STF norm in each sector,
\begin{align}
\boxed{
\sum_n\frac{Q_{2,n}^2}{\mu_n}
\le 4I_{\hat R},
}
\label{eq:m2resource}\\
\boxed{
\sum_n\frac{Q_{1,n}^2}{\mu_n}
\le 2I_{\hat R}+4Z_{\hat R},
}
\label{eq:m1resource}\\
\boxed{
\sum_n\frac{Q_{0,n}^2}{\mu_n}
\le \frac23I_{\hat R}+\frac83Z_{\hat R}.
}
\label{eq:m0resource}
\end{align}
The three right-hand sides sum to $(20/3)I_2$, recovering Eq.~\eqref{eq:qsum}. The leading far-zone resource is therefore not the complete scalar $I_2$: it is the $|m|=2$ projection controlled by the conventional inertia $I_{\hat R}$ about the propagation axis.

If the retained modal sector satisfies $\omega_n\le\Omega$, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:kappan} and \eqref{eq:qsum} still give the useful scalar corollary
\begin{equation}
\Tr(K_g^\dagger K_g)
=\sum_n\kappa_{g,n}
\le
\frac{4G}{3c^5}I_2\Omega^4 .
\label{eq:kappasum}
\end{equation}
The stronger sectorwise form is
\begin{equation}
\sum_n\omega_n^4\frac{Q_{m,n}^2}{\mu_n}
\le
\Omega^4\mathcal I_m,
\label{eq:weightedsector}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\mathcal I_2=4I_{\hat R},\qquad
\mathcal I_1=2I_{\hat R}+4Z_{\hat R},\qquad
\mathcal I_0=\frac23I_{\hat R}+\frac83Z_{\hat R}.
\end{equation}

The frequency ceiling in Eq.~\eqref{eq:retainedsector} cannot be removed using completeness alone. Equation~\eqref{eq:qsum} controls only the zeroth spectral moment of the modal quadrupole projections. Abstractly, a square-summable sequence of projection amplitudes can have a divergent fourth frequency moment; for example $a_n\propto n^{-1}$ and $\omega_n\propto n$ give $\sum_n|a_n|^2<\infty$ but $\sum_n\omega_n^4|a_n|^2=\infty$. Any whole-spectrum replacement of Eq.~\eqref{eq:kappasum} therefore requires additional elastic regularity, a microscopic cutoff, or a frequency-dependent treatment of far-detuned modes.

This last point also fixes the interpretation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:kappan}. It is an \emph{on-shell} modal linewidth evaluated at $\omega_n$. A carrier-scale Markov envelope may freeze that coupling for retained near-resonant modes. A far-detuned mode oscillating in response to a drive near $\omega_0$ must instead be treated with the frequency-dependent elastic and radiative response; assigning its on-shell $\omega_n^4$ linewidth unchanged to a low-frequency tail would exceed the validity of the Markov reduction rather than establish a physical enhancement.

The integrated-response and modal ingredients have close precedents in resonant-mass antenna theory and gravitational material-response sum rules \cite{PaikWagoner1976,Aguiar2011,Srivastava2003}. Their use here is to obtain the sector resources required by the two-ended TT propagation cut.
