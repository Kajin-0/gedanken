\section{Question and result}
\label{sec:question}

Resonant-mass gravitational antennas are historically described through elastic eigenmodes, absorption cross sections, effective coupling, directivity, and frequency-integrated response \cite{Hirakawa1976,PaikWagoner1976,Lobo1995,Aguiar2011}. Material-response dispersion relations, gravitational-wave sum rules, and complete generator--receiver calculations provide related cumulative-response viewpoints \cite{Srivastava2003,Rudenko2003}. Separately, recent work has treated gravity as a communication mediator or state-transfer channel in Newtonian and optomechanical settings \cite{KafriMilburnTaylor2015,LamiPedernalesPlenio2024,ToccaceloAndersenBrask2025,MariZippilliVitali2026}. Generic source--receiver wave-channel bounds and passive $H_2$ machinery are also established \cite{Miller2000,Molesky2020,BarasBrockett1975,Opmeer2013}.

The question here is narrower: when \emph{both} gravitational endpoints are allowed arbitrary passive linear-harmonic complexity within a specified compact narrowband modal sector, can passive resonant engineering increase the cumulative coherent transfer carried by propagating transverse-traceless radiation without bound?

Let $\omega_0$ be the absolute gravitational carrier frequency, $k_0=\omega_0/c$, and $\nu$ the complex-envelope detuning, with bandwidth $B/\omega_0\ll1$. Let $a_A$ and $a_B$ denote characteristic endpoint radii. The compact separated-wave regime used below is
\begin{equation}
k_0a_A\ll1,\qquad k_0a_B\ll1,\qquad k_0R\gg1.
\label{eq:geometryscope}
\end{equation}
Let $T(\nu)$ map selected energy-normalized local input channels at source $A$ to selected local output channels at receiver $B$, with all unobserved passive ports retained in the physical model. Define
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\equiv
\frac{1}{2\pi}
\int_{\mathcal B_\nu}\dd\nu\,
\Tr[T^\dagger(\nu)T(\nu)] .
}
\label{eq:gamma}
\end{equation}
For a scalar channel the integrand is the power transmissivity. Thus $\Gamma_{\rm coh}$ is the area under that transmissivity spectrum; for a spectrally flat input power spectral density $S_{\rm in}$ over $\mathcal B_\nu$, the transmitted output power in the same normalization is $S_{\rm in}\Gamma_{\rm coh}$. Equivalently, it is the band-limited squared $H_2$ norm of the selected transfer block. This makes it a useful cumulative-response metric: increasing a resonant peak while narrowing its bandwidth does not automatically increase $\Gamma_{\rm coh}$. It has units of inverse time, but without a noise model and signaling protocol it is neither an information capacity nor a bit rate.

Define the scalar second mass moment about each endpoint center of mass,
\begin{equation}
I_2\equiv\int_V\rho(\bm x)r^2\dd^3x .
\label{eq:I2}
\end{equation}
This is not the moment of inertia about one selected axis; the trace of the conventional inertia tensor is $2I_2$.

The main result is the following leading narrowband wave-zone bound.

\begin{theorem}[Two-ended passive gravitational spectral area]
Consider separated compact nonrelativistic linear-harmonic source and receiver systems in weak mass-quadrupole gravity satisfying Eq.~\eqref{eq:geometryscope}. At each endpoint let the complex-envelope model contain a finite or countably infinite bounded-port Markov modal sector whose retained physical modal frequencies obey
\begin{equation}
\omega_n\le\Omega,
\qquad
\Omega=\omega_0[1+O(B/\omega_0)].
\label{eq:retainedsector}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\lesssim
\frac{25G\omega_0^2}{12c^3R^2}
\min(I_{2,A},I_{2,B}) .
}
\label{eq:headline}
\end{equation}
The symbol $\lesssim$ denotes the retained narrowband, leading separated-wave-zone order. Repeated passive returns between the same two compact endpoints do not change the leading $1/R^2$ upper-bound coefficient.
\end{theorem}

The modal-sector condition in Eq.~\eqref{eq:retainedsector} is part of the theorem, not an auxiliary approximation hidden after the result. Modes with physical frequencies far above the carrier can have off-resonant tails in the operating band, but their gravitational rates scale with their own $\omega_n^4$ and are not controlled by replacing that factor with $\omega_0^4$. Such higher-frequency sectors must therefore be absent from the retained envelope model or bounded separately before Eq.~\eqref{eq:headline} is applied to them.

The physical content is the elimination of explicit passive quality factor, retained resonance count, passive unitary internal mode mixing, individual modal effective mass, optimized compact quadrupole orientation, and same-endpoint passive recurrence from the leading cumulative ceiling. These quantities can strongly reshape the transfer spectrum, but within the stated class they cannot increase its area beyond the two endpoint resources and the free-space TT channel.
