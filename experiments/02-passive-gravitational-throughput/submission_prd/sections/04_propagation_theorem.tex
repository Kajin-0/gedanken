\section{Compact TT propagation and two-ended closure}
\label{sec:propagation}

Let $q_{ij}$ be a complex STF quadrupole amplitude and $\Lambda(\hat n)$ the TT projector for direction $\hat n$. Define
\begin{equation}
F_q(\hat n)=q^*:\Lambda(\hat n):q .
\end{equation}
For an STF tensor,
\begin{equation}
F_q=q^*:q-2(q^*\hat n)\cdot(q\hat n)
+\frac12|\hat n\cdot q\hat n|^2,
\end{equation}
and isotropic angular integration gives
\begin{equation}
\int\dd\Omega\,F_q(\hat n)=\frac{8\pi}{5}q^*:q .
\label{eq:Fint}
\end{equation}
Thus the normalized quadrupole radiation patterns form a five-dimensional angular Hilbert space isometric to STF tensor space. The usual directivity ceiling follows as
\begin{equation}
D_q\le\frac52,
\end{equation}
but the final bound below does not multiply this maximum by the total endpoint quadrupole resource.

Choose the separation along $\hat z=\hat R$ and set $z=kR=\omega R/c$. Axial rotations diagonalize translation on the compact STF angular subspace into $m=0$, $|m|=1$, and $|m|=2$ sectors. Exact angular integration and separation of the outgoing $e^{iz}$ component give the squared sector singular values
\begin{align}
\eta_2(z)
&=\frac{25}{16z^{10}}
\left(z^8-2z^6+3z^4-9z^2+9\right),\\
\eta_1(z)
&=\frac{25}{4z^{10}}
\left(z^6-3z^4+36\right),\\
\eta_0(z)
&=\frac{225}{4z^{10}}
\left(z^4+3z^2+9\right).
\label{eq:finite-sector-eigenvalues}
\end{align}
The $|m|=1$ and $|m|=2$ sectors are doubly degenerate. For $z\ge3$, $\eta_2\ge\eta_1,\eta_0$. Appendix~\ref{app:finiteTT} gives the normalized kernels and outgoing amplitudes from which Eq.~\eqref{eq:finite-sector-eigenvalues} follows.

The key point is to retain these sector weights through the passive cut. Across the measured band define
\begin{equation}
\bar\eta_m(R)
=\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal B_\nu}
\eta_m\!\left(\frac{\omega(\nu)R}{c}\right).
\label{eq:etamaxband}
\end{equation}
The STF projectors $\Pi_m$ are fixed by the separation axis and do not vary with frequency. Therefore, within the outgoing compact-quadrupole model,
\begin{equation}
P_g^\dagger(\nu)P_g(\nu)
\le
\sum_{m=0}^{2}\bar\eta_m(R)\Pi_m
\end{equation}
throughout the band.

Apply Eq.~\eqref{eq:weightedcut} on the source side with the gravitational output coupling projected into each STF sector. In the elastic eigenmode basis,
\begin{equation}
\Tr\!\left[
K_{g,A}^\dagger\Pi_m K_{g,A}
\right]
=
\frac{G}{5c^5}
\sum_n\omega_n^4
\frac{Q_{m,n}^2}{\mu_n}.
\label{eq:sectortrace}
\end{equation}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:weightedsector} gives the finite-band inequality
\begin{align}
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\le\frac{G\Omega^4}{5c^5}
\Bigg[
&4\bar\eta_2 I_{\hat R,A}
+\bar\eta_1(2I_{\hat R,A}+4Z_{\hat R,A})\nonumber\\
&+\bar\eta_0\left(\frac23I_{\hat R,A}
+\frac83Z_{\hat R,A}\right)
\Bigg].
\label{eq:finitegeometryA}
\end{align}
The receiver-side weighted Gramian gives the same expression with $A\rightarrow B$. Hence
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\le
\frac{G\Omega^4}{5c^5}
\min\!\left[\mathcal G_A(R),\mathcal G_B(R)\right],
}
\label{eq:finitegeometry}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\mathcal G_X
=
4\bar\eta_2 I_{\hat R,X}
+\bar\eta_1(2I_{\hat R,X}+4Z_{\hat R,X})
+\bar\eta_0\left(\frac23I_{\hat R,X}+\frac83Z_{\hat R,X}\right).
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:finitegeometry} is a strict finite-band inequality within the retained bounded-port Markov endpoint model and the outgoing compact-quadrupole propagation model. It does not freeze $P_g$ at $\omega_0$. Its remaining approximations are the compact mass-quadrupole description and the retained endpoint model itself.

The large-$z$ behavior separates cleanly:
\begin{align}
\eta_2(z)&=\frac{25}{16z^2}\left[1+O(z^{-2})\right],\\
\eta_1(z)&=\frac{25}{4z^4}\left[1+O(z^{-2})\right],\\
\eta_0(z)&=\frac{225}{4z^6}\left[1+O(z^{-2})\right].
\end{align}
Uniformly on a fixed measured band with $\omega_->0$,
\begin{align}
\lim_{R\to\infty}R^2\bar\eta_2(R)
&=\frac{25c^2}{16\omega_-^2},\\
\lim_{R\to\infty}R^2\bar\eta_1(R)
&=\lim_{R\to\infty}R^2\bar\eta_0(R)=0.
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:finitegeometry} therefore gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\limsup_{R\rightarrow\infty}
R^2\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\le
\frac{5G\Omega^4}{4c^3\omega_-^2}
\min(I_{\hat R,A},I_{\hat R,B}),
}
\end{equation}
which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:asymptotic-headline}. The coefficient $5/4$ results from projecting the endpoint completeness relation into the propagating $|m|=2$ sector before optimization. It is therefore not the product of an independently saturated $20/3$ scalar resource and a separately optimized $5/2$ directivity.

For reference, if one discards the sector information and uses only $\eta_2\ge\eta_1,\eta_0$ together with Eq.~\eqref{eq:qsum}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:finitegeometry} reduces to the looser scalar estimate
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\le
\frac{4G\Omega^4}{3c^5}\bar\eta_2(R)
\min(I_{2,A},I_{2,B}).
\end{equation}
The sector-resolved result is always at least as strong and is strictly stronger in the leading far zone.

\subsection{Repeated passive returns}

Let $P_+$ and $P_-$ be forward and reverse separated propagation, and let $R_A,R_B$ be the gravitational reflection blocks of the passive endpoints. Repeated returns sum to
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm eff}
=(I-P_+R_AP_-R_B)^{-1}P_+ .
\end{equation}
As subblocks of passive scattering matrices, $\|R_A\|_{\op},\|R_B\|_{\op}\le1$. With $p_\pm=\|P_\pm\|_{\op}$,
\begin{equation}
\|P_{\rm eff}\|_{\op}
\le\frac{p_+}{1-p_+p_-}
\end{equation}
whenever $p_+p_-<1$. This is an exact resolvent estimate, not a small-$p$ expansion. In the far zone $p_\pm=O(R^{-1})$, so the round-trip loop is $O(R^{-2})$ in amplitude and the first possible correction to single-pass power is $O(R^{-4})$. Endpoint reflections may mix STF sectors at finite distance, but they cannot modify the leading $R^{-2}$ single-pass coefficient in Eq.~\eqref{eq:asymptotic-headline}. Destructive interference may instead reduce the realized transfer. The scattering composition is standard network theory \cite{Redheffer1962}.
