Please consider the manuscript **“An Inertia-Controlled Spectral-Area Bound for Passive Far-Zone Gravitational Transduction”** for publication as a **Research Article** in *Physical Review D*.

The paper derives a frequency-integrated upper bound on coherent transfer between two separated compact passive matter systems coupled through propagating linearized gravity. For the selected narrowband transfer matrix `T(nu)`, we define

```math
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathcal B_\nu}
\operatorname{Tr}[T^\dagger(\nu)T(\nu)]\,d\nu.
```

Within the stated retained passive endpoint and outgoing compact-quadrupole TT model, the rigorous far-zone result is

```math
\limsup_{R\to\infty}R^2\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\le
\frac{5G\Omega^4}{4c^3\omega_-^2}
\min(I_{\hat R,A},I_{\hat R,B}),
```

where `I_Rhat` is the conventional moment of inertia of each endpoint about the source-receiver axis. For a retained carrier-scale narrow band this becomes

```math
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\lesssim
\frac{5G\omega_0^2}{4c^3R^2}
\min(I_{\hat R,A},I_{\hat R,B}).
```

The metric is the band-limited squared `H2` spectral area of the selected transfer block: it measures cumulative power transmissivity across the operating band rather than peak resonant height. The conceptual result is that passive resonant complexity can redistribute gravitational transfer in frequency but cannot raise the leading two-ended spectral-area ceiling beyond the smaller separation-axis endpoint resource and the propagating TT channel. Passive quality factor, retained resonance count, unitary internal mode mixing, and same-two-endpoint recurrence do not appear in the leading ceiling within the theorem class.

We view *Physical Review D* as the natural venue because the result is a gravitation and gravitational-wave propagation theorem rather than a detector-sensitivity calculation or information-capacity result. A deliberately generous scale estimate makes the limitation explicit: for equal uniform-sphere endpoints with `M=1000 kg`, `a=1 m`, `f_0=1 kHz`, and `k_0R=100`, the leading ceiling is approximately `2.15e-39 s^-1`. The paper is therefore presented as a structural passive-resource/no-go result, not a near-term experimental proposal.

The result builds on historical resonant-mass response, gravitational-antenna modal theory, material-response sum rules, classical matching and antenna limits, passive `H2` mathematics, wave-channel bounds, and multiple-scattering theory. Recent work also establishes gravity-mediated communication and state-transfer questions in Newtonian and optomechanical settings. The specific contribution claimed here is narrower: a passive far-zone TT spectral-area closure in which the endpoint response is reduced to geometry-resolved inertia resources before propagation is closed. We found no exact equivalent complete theorem in the primary literature inspected; no priority claim is made.
