Please consider the manuscript **“An Inertia-Controlled Spectral-Area Bound for Passive Far-Zone Gravitational Transduction”** for publication as a **Research Article** in *Physical Review D*.

The paper derives a frequency-integrated upper bound on coherent transfer between two separated compact passive matter systems coupled through propagating linearized gravity. For a narrowband complex-envelope transfer matrix $T(\nu)$, the main result is

```math
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\lesssim
\frac{25G\omega_0^2}{12c^3R^2}\min(I_{2,A},I_{2,B}),
```

where $I_2=\int\rho r^2d^3x$ is the scalar second mass moment of each endpoint. The theorem applies within an explicitly delimited weak-field, nonrelativistic, leading-quadrupole, compact-endpoint, separated-wave-zone, passive linear-harmonic class and to a retained carrier-scale modal sector.

The metric is the band-limited $H_2$ spectral area of the selected transfer block: it measures cumulative power transmissivity across the operating band rather than peak resonant height. The conceptual result is that passive resonant complexity can redistribute gravitational transfer in frequency but cannot increase its leading two-ended spectral-area ceiling beyond the smaller endpoint gravitational resource and the compact transverse-traceless propagation channel. Quality factor, retained resonance count, passive unitary mode mixing, individual modal effective masses, and same-two-endpoint recurrence disappear from the leading cumulative ceiling within the theorem class.

We view *Physical Review D* as the natural venue because the result is a gravitation and gravitational-wave propagation theorem rather than a detector-sensitivity calculation or information-capacity result. A worked scale estimate in the manuscript makes the limitation explicit: for a deliberately generous 1000 kg, 1 m uniform sphere at 1 kHz, choosing $k_0R=100$ gives a ceiling of order $5\times10^{-39}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$. The paper is therefore presented as a conceptual passive-resource/no-go result, not a near-term experimental proposal.
