\title{An Inertia-Controlled Spectral-Area Bound for Passive Far-Zone Gravitational Transduction}

\author{Terence Fisher}
\email{terence@brooks-photonics.com}
\affiliation{Brooks Photonics}

\date{August 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We derive a frequency-integrated upper bound on coherent transfer between two separated compact passive matter systems coupled through propagating linearized gravity. For a narrowband complex-envelope transfer matrix $T(\nu)$, define
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\rm coh}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathcal B_\nu}\dd\nu\,\Tr[T^\dagger(\nu)T(\nu)].
\end{equation}
For energy-normalized ports this is the band-limited $H_2$ spectral area: the integral of power transmissivity over detuning. Passivity reduces the problem to gravitational output couplings of either endpoint. Instead of bounding the complete quadrupole strength and propagation directivity separately, we resolve the five-dimensional symmetric-trace-free quadrupole space into the $m=0$, $|m|=1$, and $|m|=2$ sectors defined by the separation axis. Mass-weighted completeness then gives sectorwise endpoint resources. Combining them with the exact outgoing compact-quadrupole propagation singular values yields a finite-band, finite-$kR$ inequality within that compact angular model, without freezing the propagator at the carrier. In the far zone only the $|m|=2$ sector survives at order $R^{-2}$, giving
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\lesssim
\frac{5G\omega_0^2}{4c^3R^2}
\min(I_{\hat R,A},I_{\hat R,B})
}
\end{equation}
in the leading narrowband form, where $I_{\hat R}=\int\rho |\hat R\times\bm x|^2\dd^3x$ is the ordinary moment of inertia about the line joining the endpoints. The rigorous asymptotic statement retains the upper modal frequency $\Omega$ and the lowest physical frequency in the measured band. The result applies to a finite or countably infinite bounded-port Markov modal sector retained near the carrier. Completeness alone cannot control an unrestricted all-frequency Markov trace; far-detuned sectors require a frequency-dependent treatment or additional material regularity. Passive internal mode mixing and repeated passive returns between the same compact endpoints do not change the leading coefficient.
\end{abstract}
