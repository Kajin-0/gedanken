# External Review Outreach

## Recommended subject

**Adversarial check of a passive gravitational-transduction bound**

Alternative:

**Could you try to break this gravitational antenna bound?**

## Core email

Professor / Dr. [Name],

I have a short theoretical manuscript on passive gravitational transduction and am looking for an adversarial technical check before submission.

The result is a frequency-integrated bound for two separated compact passive matter systems,

```math
\Gamma_{\rm coh}
\lesssim
\frac{25G\omega^2}{12c^3R^2}\min(I_A,I_B).
```

The individual ingredients are deliberately **not** claimed as new: resonant-mass antenna theory, reciprocity/directivity, modal completeness, passive `H2` identities, and source--receiver wave-channel methods are all credited as prior art. The only candidate contribution is the final two-ended inertia closure.

I am not looking for an endorsement. I would specifically value one of three things:

1. a counterexample or normalization/assumption error;
2. an older primary source that already contains the equivalent two-ended theorem; or
3. your judgment that the closure is too immediate from established results to constitute a useful standalone result.

I attached / linked a one-page technical summary and the manuscript. If you only have time to inspect the few equations relevant to your area, that would already be very useful.

Thank you for considering it.

Best,
[Name]

# What to attach/link

Keep the first contact small:

1. `TECHNICAL_SUMMARY.md` or a rendered one-page version.
2. `NOVELTY_AND_SCOPE.md`.
3. Manuscript PDF.

Do **not** attach the entire audit archive unless requested.

If the reviewer responds positively, then send `AUDIT_EQUATIONS_AND_ASSUMPTIONS.md` and the relevant audit file.

# Response discipline

If a reviewer identifies a defect, do not defend the manuscript immediately. First reduce the objection to a precise statement:

```text
What equation/assumption fails?
Under what physical regime?
Is the objection inside the declared scope?
Does it change the coefficient, scaling, or theorem class?
```

Only then reopen the derivation.
