## 1. Generic quantum-transducer capacity

### Wang, Li, Jiang — Quantum capacities of transducers

Nature Communications 13, 6698 (2022).

This work provides the closest general information-theory language:

- continuous-time capacity is integrated over frequency;
- efficiency, bandwidth, and noise enter jointly;
- generic coupled-bosonic transducers admit capacity bounds controlled by a coherent coupling scale.

Collision assessment:

**Important framework prior art, but not the same physical bound.** Their resource ceiling is a transducer coupling scale; Experiment 02 attempts to derive the gravitational coupling resource itself from passive mass-quadrupole spectral weight.

URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-34373-8

## 2. Passive transducer efficiency-bandwidth limits

### Shi, Zhuang — Overcoming the fundamental limit of quantum transduction via intraband entanglement

Optica Quantum 2, 475 (2024); arXiv:2404.09441.

This work explicitly studies an integrated efficiency-bandwidth quantity for passive cavity transducers and shows that an additional entanglement resource can overcome the passive architecture limit.

Collision assessment:

**Strong conceptual prior art for the type of metric and passive/resource-assisted distinction.** The physical bound is tied to the specific electro-optic/electro-optomechanical coupling architecture, not to gravitational quadrupole oscillator strength.

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09441

## 3. Passive linear quantum-network formalism

### Guță, Yamamoto — System identification for passive linear quantum systems

Phys. Rev. A 89, 032103 (2014); arXiv:1303.3771.

This paper gives the standard passive input-output model used in Experiment 02,

```math
A=-i\Omega-\frac12C^\dagger C,
```

and the complete transfer matrix

```math
\Xi(s)=I-C(sI-A)^{-1}C^\dagger,
```

which is unitary on the real-frequency axis when all passive channels are retained.

Collision assessment:

**Formalism prior art.** The passive-network identities themselves are not claimed new. The candidate contribution is their gravity-specific cut-set use together with the microscopic quadrupole spectral bound.

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.3771

## 4. Graviton absorption by ordinary matter

### Boughn, Rothman — Aspects of Graviton Detection

arXiv:gr-qc/0605052 (2006).

Computes graviton emission and absorption by atomic hydrogen and discusses subtleties of the linearized gravitational interaction Hamiltonian.

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0605052

### Palessandro, Sloth — Gravitational Absorption Lines

Phys. Rev. D 101, 043504 (2020); arXiv:1910.01657.

Shows that graviton absorption by ordinary bound states is extremely weak in standard gravity and discusses Planck-scale absorption cross sections.

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01657

Collision assessment:

**Microscopic absorption prior art, not an integrated transducer throughput theorem.** These works reinforce the physical smallness of ordinary-matter gravitational coupling but do not inspect both material interfaces in a passive spectral cut-set construction.

## 5. Current gravitational sensor limits

### Gaudio — Fundamental Limits of Quantum Sensors for Gravitational Wave Detection

Classical and Quantum Gravity (2026); arXiv:2603.06772.

This current work compares different gravitational-wave coupling mechanisms and shows that detector viability is controlled by the physical coupling mechanism before quantum sensor improvements are considered. It distinguishes what gravity permits from what a detector noise architecture permits.

Collision assessment:

**Closest current “fundamental limits” framing found, but different quantity and problem.** It concerns strain-to-observable transducer gains and detector sensitivity/noise, not source-to-receiver quantum transmission, a passive efficiency-bandwidth integral, or a quadrupole EWSR cut set.

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06772
