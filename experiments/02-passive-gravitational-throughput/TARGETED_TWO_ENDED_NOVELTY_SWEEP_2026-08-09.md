## 1. Historical resonant gravitational antennas

Classical resonant-mass gravitational-wave antenna theory already contains the one-sided physics that increasing resonant quality factor raises peak response while narrowing bandwidth, leaving a finite integrated response. Paik and Wagoner (Phys. Rev. D 13, 2694, 1976) and the later resonant-bar literature therefore block any novelty claim based merely on a Q-independent integrated gravitational absorption response.

Recent resonant-detector work likewise treats bandwidth engineering as established detector physics. None of the inspected sources in this family was found to formulate a two-ended passive quantum cut set in which independent source and receiver gravitational spectral resources are connected by a normalized propagating TT singular channel.

**Collision status:** one-ended ingredient is established; no inspected two-ended theorem collision found.

## 2. Susceptibility / microscopic material response

Therefore Experiment 02 must not claim novelty for using a spectral material response or for replacing a single mechanical resonance by a response function.

The current Experiment 02 distinction is narrower: it uses a quadrupole EWSR to close the endpoint coupling trace that appears in a passive end-to-end transfer cut set, at both ends of a propagating link.

**Collision status:** material-response language established; no inspected source found closing both endpoints through the same end-to-end transfer theorem.

## 4. Modern quantum-transducer efficiency/bandwidth and capacity

Wang, Li, and Jiang, *Quantum capacities of transducers* (Nature Communications 13, 6698, 2022; arXiv:2203.00012), define continuous-time quantum capacity by integrating frequency-resolved channel capacities and show that efficiency, bandwidth, and noise must be treated jointly.

Shi and Zhuang, *Overcoming the fundamental limit of quantum transduction via intraband entanglement* (Optica Quantum 2, 475, 2024; arXiv:2404.09441), derive efficiency-bandwidth limits for passive electro-optic/electro-optomechanical transducers and show how an additional nonclassical resource can exceed those passive architecture limits.

These works strongly overlap with the metric philosophy of Experiment 02. Their resource ceilings are supplied by device coupling/pump scales; they do not, in the inspected versions, derive a gravity-specific endpoint oscillator-strength ceiling at both matter interfaces and connect those ceilings through compact TT free-space propagation.

**Collision status:** efficiency-bandwidth/capacity framework established; no inspected gravitational two-ended material-resource theorem found.

## 5. Two-detector gravitational-wave literature

Classical two-detector gravitational-wave work studies antenna overlap, orientation, separation, stochastic-background correlation, and sensitivity-bandwidth tradeoffs. Christensen, Phys. Rev. D 46, 5250 (1992), is an example of established two-antenna correlation physics.

This literature is important because it prevents any broad claim that coupling two separated gravitational antennas or optimizing their relative geometry is new. However, the inspected two-detector work concerns detection/correlation of an externally supplied gravitational field rather than a source matter system whose own passive gravitational spectral resource is included as the first cut of the same end-to-end transfer channel.

**Collision status:** two-detector propagation/overlap physics established; no inspected source-to-matter-resource/receiver-resource H2 theorem found.
