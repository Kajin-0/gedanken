\section{Approximation hierarchy}
\label{sec:error-budget}

The source, propagation, and receiver approximations are controlled by distinct small parameters. Table~\ref{tab:error-budget} collects the leading corrections in one place. Several entries are not merely estimated errors: the finite-spoke dependence is retained explicitly within the linear elastic model, the controller propagation form factor is known through $O(q_c^2)$, and the TT propagation kernel is known in closed form.

For the source one requires
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_u,\ q,\ q_c,\ \beta,\ \Ccal\ll1,
\end{equation}
while the dynamical model additionally requires
\begin{equation}
B/\omega\ll1.
\end{equation}
The finite-hub term is architecture dependent rather than universal. A sufficient design condition is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\epsilon_h
=\frac{M_h}{\mu}
\frac{r_h}{L}
\frac{u_h}{u}
\ll1.}
\label{eq:hub-design}
\end{equation}
For the symmetric quadratic controller bus itself, the branch-conditioned fields obey $\Phi_-=-\Phi_+$, so the local controller energy density is branch common and contributes no branch-odd quadrupole at this order. A non-cleared controller excitation is instead an explicit source loss or dephasing channel.

The independent TT mode-overlap calculation in Appendix~\ref{app:tt-overlap} gives the exact propagation factor
\begin{equation}
|t_{BA}^{\rm TT}|^2
=\frac{25}{16z^2}
\left(1-\frac{2}{z^2}+\frac{3}{z^4}-\frac{9}{z^6}+\frac{9}{z^8}\right),
\qquad z=kR.
\label{eq:exact-prop-prob}
\end{equation}
Thus at the benchmark value $kR=10$, replacing the exact TT factor by $25/[16(kR)^2]$ overestimates the propagation contribution by approximately $1.97\%$, without affecting the qualitative interface-dominated conclusion.
