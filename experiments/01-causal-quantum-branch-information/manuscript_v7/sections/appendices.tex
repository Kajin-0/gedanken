\section{Branch-distance conservation at the encoder handoff}
\label{app:handoff}

For the damped two-mode modal encoder, the source amplitude is
\begin{equation}
\alpha_s(t)=-is\zeta\frac{g}{\Omega}\ee^{-\kappa_A t/4}\sin(\Omega t).
\end{equation}
The branch separation of the source mode is $2\alpha_+(t)$. Each Markov output port $j$ carries branch-difference amplitude $2\sqrt{\kappa_j}\alpha_+(t)$. The branch distance emitted into port $j$ through the controller-empty modal time is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
N_{\Delta,j}^{\rm enc}
=4\frac{\kappa_j}{\kappa_A}|\zeta|^2
\left(1-\ee^{-\kappa_A T_*/2}\right).}
\end{equation}
The source branch distance remaining at handoff is
\begin{equation}
N_{\Delta,A}(T_*)=4|\zeta|^2\ee^{-\kappa_A T_*/2}.
\end{equation}
Summing all modal source output ports gives
\begin{equation}
N_{\Delta,A}(T_*)+
\sum_jN_{\Delta,j}^{\rm enc}=4|\zeta|^2,
\end{equation}
which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:distance-conservation}. In the fast-encoder limit, the emitted precursor fraction is
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{\rm pre}
=1-\ee^{-\kappa_A T_*/2}
\simeq\frac{\pi\kappa_A}{4g}.
\end{equation}
In the literal local controller-field completion, the same norm accounting is applied to the enlarged coherent mode vector that also contains controller-bus modes. A post-handoff virtual bosonic channel is defined only after those branch-dependent controller modes have cleared or been coherently reabsorbed.

\section{Full-system Poincar\'e-charge audit}
\label{app:charges}

Use the center-of-energy first moment
\begin{equation}
D^i=\frac1{c^2}\int\dd^3x\,x^iT^{00}
\end{equation}
instead of fixing a boost-generator sign convention. At fixed total momentum, equality of $D^i$ is equivalent to equality of the boost charges.

The branch symmetry of each subsystem is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:charges}. The crucial field entries use the fact that the two branch-dependent coherent amplitudes are related by a global sign after common displacements are removed, while the corresponding free-field Poincar\'e generators are quadratic. Field charges can therefore be nonzero for an individual pulse while still being exactly equal between branches at the retained order.

\label{tab:charges}

For the endpoint and spoke sectors, rest and kinetic/strain energies are branch even, opposite radial pairs cancel linear momentum and center-of-energy first moment, and the radial motion has zero orbital angular momentum. The hub remains at the origin. The compact work system has the same free energy and momentum in both logical sectors at the audited order.

For the propagating controller field, the two branch solutions satisfy
\begin{equation}
\Phi_-=-\Phi_+
\end{equation}
after common fields are removed. Its free Poincar\'e generators are quadratic and therefore sign invariant. The local elastic--controller interaction is also branch common pointwise because Eqs.~\eqref{eq:phiret}--\eqref{eq:eigenstrain} give
\begin{equation}
\left[
\partial_x\xi_a^{(s)}-
\epsilon_a\lambda\chi\Phi_a^{(s)}
\right]^2
=
\left[u f_q'-\lambda\chi X_C(t-x/v_c)\right]^2.
\end{equation}
Thus the controller field may carry a distinguishable branch record during the local operation without carrying different total Poincar\'e charges in the two branches.

For the emitted graviton and other linear fields,
\begin{equation}
\alpha_-=-\alpha_+,
\end{equation}
and any quadratic free-field charge is invariant under this sign transformation. Radiation recoil is consequently branch common as well.

Let $Q_A$ denote any of the ten total Poincar\'e generators. The reference stress-energy is proportional to the identity on its degenerate logical doublet, so
\begin{equation}
\langle\mathsf0|Q_A|\mathsf1\rangle=0.
\end{equation}
The table establishes equality of the two diagonal elements. Hence
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
V_{\mathcal C}^\dagger Q_A V_{\mathcal C}=q_A I_{\mathcal C},}
\end{equation}
which gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:charge-code}. This is a first-order/working-model statement; branch-dependent material asymmetry, a nondegenerate reference stress tensor, controller nonlinearities/losses, or higher-order gravitational self-interaction can violate it.

\section{Exact pure-loss partial-transpose block}
\label{app:pureloss}

For Eq.~\eqref{eq:hybrid}, define normalized receiver even and odd cat states
\begin{equation}
|C_+\rangle=\frac{|B\rangle+|-B\rangle}{\sqrt{2(1+s_B)}},
\qquad
|C_-\rangle=\frac{|B\rangle-|-B\rangle}{\sqrt{2(1-s_B)}}.
\end{equation}
The partially transposed state decomposes into two $2\times2$ blocks. The block containing the negative eigenvalue is
\begin{equation}
M_-=
\frac14
\begin{pmatrix}
(1+s_B)(1-s_E) & \sqrt{1-s_B^2}(1+s_E)\\
\sqrt{1-s_B^2}(1+s_E) & (1-s_B)(1-s_E)
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Its determinant is
\begin{equation}
\det M_-=-\frac{s_E(1-s_B^2)}{4}<0
\end{equation}
for every $A>0$ and $\eta>0$. The magnitude of its negative eigenvalue gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:exactN}.

\section{Passive quadrupole sum-rule bound}
\label{app:sumrule}

Energy-weighted sum rules may be written as double commutators, and coordinate-multipole/E2 applications are standard \cite{LuJohnson2018,Hinohara2019}. Let a stationary nonrelativistic matter system have Hamiltonian eigenstates $|m\rangle$ with energies $E_m$ and a passive diagonal state $\rho=\sum_m p_m|m\rangle\langle m|$, so $p_m\ge p_n$ whenever $E_m<E_n$. For any Hermitian operator $F$,
\begin{equation}
\frac12\Tr\rho[F,[H,F]]
=\sum_{m<n}(p_m-p_n)(E_n-E_m)|F_{mn}|^2.
\end{equation}
For the five orthonormal STF mass-quadrupole components $Q_A$, the coordinate-space commutator identity gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\sum_A\frac12\Tr\rho[Q_A,[H,Q_A]]
=\frac{10}{3}\hbar^2\langle I\rangle_\rho,}
\end{equation}
where $I=\sum_a m_a r_a^2$. Passivity makes every positive-frequency term on the spectral side nonnegative. For a narrow selected band near $\omega$,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{\rm band,A}(p_m-p_n)|Q^A_{mn}|^2
\lesssim\frac{10}{3}\frac{\hbar\langle I\rangle}{\omega}.
\end{equation}
Using the quadrupole graviton transition rate
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{nm}^{(g)}
=\frac{2G\omega_{nm}^5}{5\hbar c^5}
Q_{ij}^{nm}Q_{ij}^{mn},
\end{equation}
one obtains the passive net gravitational linewidth ceiling
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{g,\rm net}
\lesssim\frac{4G}{3c^5}\langle I\rangle\omega^4.
\end{equation}
Writing $\langle I\rangle=ML^2$, $\Ccal=2GM/(c^2L)$, and $\beta=\omega L/c$ gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\frac{\kappa_{g,\rm net}}{\omega}
\lesssim\frac23\Ccal\beta^3,}
\end{equation}
which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:sumrule}. The positivity step is exactly where the passive-state assumption enters; active or inverted states are not constrained by this positive-term argument. Prior graviton-absorption studies already show extremely small ordinary-matter cross sections by complementary methods \cite{BoughnRothman2006,PalessandroSloth2020}.

\section{Passive source broadening}
\label{app:broadening}

For a passive exponential source with $r=\kappa_A/\kappa_B$, fixed intrinsic source gravitational rate $\kappa_{g,A}$, and fixed receiver parameters, the optimized end-to-end transfer is
\begin{equation}
\tau_{A\to B}^{\max}(r)
=4\frac{\kappa_{g,A}\kappa_\Delta}{\kappa_B^2}
r^{2r/(1-r)}.
\end{equation}
Since
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dd}{\dd r}\ln r^{2r/(1-r)}
=\frac{2(\ln r+1-r)}{(1-r)^2}<0,
\end{equation}
adding passive nongravitational source damping strictly reduces the source-resolved optimum. Receiver-local temporal matching and source branching must therefore not be optimized independently.
