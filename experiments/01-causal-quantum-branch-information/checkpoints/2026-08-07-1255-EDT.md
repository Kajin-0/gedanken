## Exact scalar-field influence-functional structure

Use a Gaussian massless scalar mediator with forward/backward total currents

$$
J_1=J_A^{(L)}+j_B[q],
\qquad
J_2=J_A^{(R)}+j_B[q'].
$$

Define

$$
J^-=J_1-J_2,
\qquad
J^+=\frac{J_1+J_2}{2},
$$

and

$$
\Delta J_A=J_A^{(L)}-J_A^{(R)},
\qquad
\bar J_A=\frac{J_A^{(L)}+J_A^{(R)}}2,
$$

$$
j_B^-=j_B[q]-j_B[q'],
\qquad
j_B^+=\frac{j_B[q]+j_B[q']}{2}.
$$

Then

$$
J^-=\Delta J_A+j_B^-,
\qquad
J^+=\bar J_A+j_B^+.
$$

In natural units and with the noise-kernel normalization absorbed into $G_H$, the Feynman-Vernon influence functional has the structural form

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal F_{LR}[q,q']
=
\exp\left[
 i\,J^-G_RJ^+
-
\frac12J^-G_HJ^-
\right].
}
$$

The compact off-diagonal expression is therefore

$$
\boxed{
\ln\mathcal F_{LR}
=
 i(\Delta J_A+j_B^-)G_R(\bar J_A+j_B^+)
-
\frac12(\Delta J_A+j_B^-)G_H(\Delta J_A+j_B^-).
}
$$

### Diagonal conditional probe channels

For $\Phi_s$, the source branch is the same on the forward and backward histories. Thus the source-dependent deterministic probe term is

$$
j_B^-G_RJ_A^{(s)}.
$$

The difference between the two conditional probe channels is therefore controlled by

$$
\boxed{j_B^-G_R\Delta J_A.}
$$

Because $G_R$ is retarded, this branch-dependent probe response vanishes outside causal contact.

### Off-diagonal history coherence

For $\Xi$, the noise/decoherence part contains

$$
\boxed{
\frac12(\Delta J_A+j_B^-)G_H(\Delta J_A+j_B^-).
}
$$

This is a major conceptual result: the environment distinguishes the **complete source-plus-probe history difference**, not the source alone.

Expanding,

$$
\frac12\Delta J_AG_H\Delta J_A
+
\Delta J_AG_Hj_B^-
+
\frac12j_B^-G_Hj_B^-.
$$

The cross term means that branch information recorded in the probe and branch information recorded in the mediator/environment are correlated. Simply computing the source-only term $\Delta J_AG_H\Delta J_A/2$ can overestimate irreversible decoherence because an appropriate probe erasure may recover part of that history coherence.

### Field-theory meaning of the quantum eraser

At influence-functional level, an optimal eraser seeks to make the two **complete** histories less distinguishable to the unobserved field. Schematically it tries to reduce the noise-kernel norm

$$
\|\Delta J_A+j_B^-\|_{G_H}^2.
$$

This is the continuous-field counterpart of the previously derived complementary-output fidelity $C_\Xi$.

### Response versus history leakage

The desired probe record comes from the retarded cross-kernel

$$
G_R\Delta J_A,
$$

while the loss of recoverable history coherence is governed by the Hadamard/noise sector. Thus the history-transfer margin has the conceptual form

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal M_\Xi
\sim
\text{retarded branch record delivered to }B
-
\text{unrecoverable complementary branch record}.
}
$$
