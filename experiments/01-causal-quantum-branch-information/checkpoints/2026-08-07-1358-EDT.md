## New result: long-time history-transfer rate

For the scalar toy model, define the complementary record spectrum $S_E(\omega)$ by

$$
2\Gamma_\Xi
=\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}
S_E(\omega)|\widetilde u(\omega)|^2,
$$

and the source-to-probe frequency response

$$
\mathcal R_B(\omega_B,R)
=\frac{\lambda_Bx_{\rm zpf}}{\hbar}
\int d^3x\,G_R(\omega_B;\mathbf z_B,\mathbf x)s(\mathbf x).
$$

For an interaction window

$$
\tau=T-R/c
$$

long enough that the response is narrow around $\omega_B$,

$$
\boxed{
\eta_T
\simeq
\tau\,\gamma_{\rm hist}(R,\omega_B),
}
$$

with

$$
\boxed{
\gamma_{\rm hist}(R,\omega_B)
=\frac{|\mathcal R_B(\omega_B,R)|^2}{S_E(\omega_B)}.
}
$$

The optimized strong witness can become positive when $\eta_T>1$, giving the approximate onset

$$
\boxed{
T_*(R)
\simeq
\frac{R}{c}+\gamma_{\rm hist}^{-1}(R,\omega_B).
}
$$

The nonclassicality front therefore has a light-travel delay plus a coherent buildup delay.

## Source-amplitude cancellation

In the linear regime, both the useful branch-transfer exponent and complementary leakage exponent are quadratic in the overall source-history amplitude. Therefore the sign threshold is a property of the **channel efficiency**, not source strength. More source mass/charge/separation increases observable magnitude but not the ideal fraction of branch information delivered to the intended receiver.

This explains the source-mass cancellation previously found in the gravity radiation-limited threshold.

## Pure-loss analogy

If a branch-dependent bosonic displacement is passively split so that fraction $\tau_{\rm ch}$ reaches the probe,

$$
\mathcal M_\Xi=(2\tau_{\rm ch}-1)|\Delta|^2.
$$

Thus the strong history-transfer witness is positive iff

$$
\tau_{\rm ch}>1/2.
$$

This coincides with the familiar pure-loss bosonic degradable/antidegradable boundary. The witness is sufficient, not necessary: source-probe entanglement may exist below $1/2$.

Interpretation: $\mathcal M_\Xi>0$ demands that the intended probe receive a better branch record than the unobserved complementary output.

## Gravity scaling implication

In the Newtonian near zone,

$$
\Delta F_B\propto R^{-3},
$$

so the useful branch-transfer exponent scales as $R^{-6}$. Source-generated fundamental leakage does not acquire the same receiver-distance suppression. Schematically,

$$
\boxed{
\gamma_{\rm hist}^{(G)}\propto R^{-6},
\qquad
T_*(R)-R/c\propto R^6.
}
$$

This exposes a severe causality-versus-coupling tradeoff: larger $R$ makes retardation easier to resolve while rapidly suppressing the near-field quantum interaction.

A full gravity result must include the conserved stress-energy of the source plus actuator/apparatus before this is promoted beyond schematic near-zone scaling.
