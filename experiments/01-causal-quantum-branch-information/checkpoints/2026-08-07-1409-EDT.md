## Equivalence-principle correction

A self-contained freely falling receiver should not be modeled as a single mass responding to a uniform gravitational force. Its leading local observable is geodesic deviation / curvature.

In Fermi normal coordinates,

$$
H_{\rm curv}
=\frac{\mu_Bc^2}{2}R_{0i0j}\xi^i\xi^j.
$$

For a differential mode with equilibrium baseline $L_B$ and quantum coordinate $x_B$,

$$
\boxed{
H_{\rm drive}
=\mu_BL_B\mathcal E_{nn}x_B,
\qquad
\mathcal E_{ij}=c^2R_{0i0j}.
}
$$

This is the clean gauge-invariant receiver used going forward.

## Wave-zone asymptotic

Far-zone curvature is

$$
\mathcal E_{ij}^{\rm GW}
=-\frac{G}{c^4R}Q_{ij}^{(4),TT}.
$$

For a transverse receiver and dimensionless angular factor $\mathcal A$,

$$
\mathcal C_{\rm hist,tidal}^{\rm WZ}
\simeq
\frac{15\mathcal A^2}{4}
\frac{G\mu_BL_B^2Q_B\omega_B}{c^3R^2}.
$$

Define

$$
\epsilon=\omega_BR/c,
\qquad
\nu_G=G\mu_BL_B^2Q_B\omega_B^3/c^5.
$$

Then

$$
\mathcal C_{\rm hist}^{\rm NZ}
\sim540\nu_G\epsilon^{-10},
$$

while

$$
\mathcal C_{\rm hist}^{\rm WZ}
\sim(15\mathcal A^2/4)\nu_G\epsilon^{-2}.
$$

For laboratory systems $\nu_G\ll1$, so a local strong-history witness is naturally deep near-zone, whereas obvious retardation is wave-zone.

This is a **strong-witness local-receiver limitation**, not a no-go theorem for entanglement.
