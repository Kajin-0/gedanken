## Conserved quadrupole gravity reduction

Use a compact source with branch-difference STF quadrupole

$$
\Delta Q_{ij}(t)
=q(t)\left(n_in_j-\frac13\delta_{ij}\right).
$$

On-axis, the Newtonian quadrupole potential difference scales as

$$
\Delta\Phi_Q\sim\frac{Gq}{R^3},
$$

so a radial probe experiences branch force difference

$$
\boxed{
\Delta F_B\sim\frac{3Gm_Bq}{R^4}.
}
$$

The coherent probe response per quadrupole amplitude is therefore

$$
|\mathcal R_B^{(G)}|
\sim
\frac{3Gm_Bx_{\rm zpf}}{\hbar R^4}.
$$

The quadrupole radiation formula gives a branch-distinguishing graviton record spectrum

$$
S_G(\omega)
\sim
\frac{2G}{15\hbar c^5}\omega^5
$$

for the stated axisymmetric convention.

Hence the narrow-band history-transfer rate scales as

$$
\boxed{
\gamma_{\rm hist}^{(G)}
\sim
\frac{135}{4}
\frac{Gm_Bc^5}{R^8\omega_B^6},
}
$$

and with probe damping

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal C_{\rm hist}^{(G)}
\sim
\frac{135}{4}
\frac{Gm_Bc^5}
{\kappa_BR^8\omega_B^6}.
}
$$

The source quadrupole amplitude cancels from this ideal efficiency ratio.

### Critical radius

The radiation-only strong-witness threshold $\mathcal C_{\rm hist}^{(G)}=1$ gives

$$
R_c
\sim
\left[
\frac{135}{4}
\frac{Gm_Bc^5}{\kappa_B\omega_B^6}
\right]^{1/8}.
$$

Illustrative values:

- $m_B=1\,\mathrm g$, $f_B=1\,\mathrm{Hz}$, $Q_B=10^8$: $R_c\sim14\,\mathrm{km}$;
- $m_B=1\,\mathrm{kg}$, $f_B=100\,\mathrm{Hz}$, $Q_B=10^6$: $R_c\sim330\,\mathrm m$.

These are efficiency ratios only; absolute gravitational signals may still be unmeasurably small.

### Near-zone / wave-zone tension

Define

$$
\epsilon=\frac{\omega_BR}{c},
\qquad
\mu_G=\frac{Gm_BQ_B\omega_B}{c^3}.
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal C_{\rm hist}^{(G)}
\sim
\frac{135}{4}\mu_G\epsilon^{-8}.
}
$$

For laboratory systems $\mu_G\ll1$, so the local-probe strong-witness regime is parametrically near-zone, $\epsilon\ll1$.

This reveals a causal tradeoff:

- near field: reactive coherent transfer dominates, but retardation is dynamically tiny;
- wave zone: retardation is visible, but branch information propagates into many gravitational modes and a local receiver captures only a small fraction.

A full wave-zone TT calculation is now required.

### Gedanken resolution

An ideal spherical quantum receiver matched to the outgoing quadrupolar mode could, in principle, collect more than half of the branch-distinguishing radiation. In the pure-loss benchmark the strong history margin becomes positive for collection efficiency $\tau_{\rm ch}>1/2$. This separates an ideal causal-information Gedanken version from the near-field local-probe version.
