## Exact gauge-invariant quadrupole crossover

Choose

$$
Q_{xx}=q(t),\qquad Q_{yy}=-q(t),\qquad Q_{zz}=0,
$$

place the receiver on the $z$ axis, and use an $x$-directed freely falling differential mode.

Direct evaluation of the linearized Riemann tensor gives

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{xx}(t,R)
=
-\frac{G}{R^5}
\left[
3q
+\frac{3R}{c}\dot q
+\frac{3R^2}{c^2}\ddot q
+\frac{2R^3}{c^3}q^{(3)}
+\frac{R^4}{c^4}q^{(4)}
\right]_{t-R/c}.
}
$$

For $q(t)=q_\omega e^{-i\omega t}$ and $\epsilon=\omega R/c$,

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal E_{xx}(\omega,R)
=-\frac{Gq_\omega}{R^5}P(\epsilon)e^{i\epsilon},
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
P(\epsilon)=3-3i\epsilon-3\epsilon^2+2i\epsilon^3+\epsilon^4,
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
|P(\epsilon)|^2
=\epsilon^8-2\epsilon^6+3\epsilon^4-9\epsilon^2+9.
}
$$

This single transfer function contains:

- static tidal field: $\mathcal E\sim-3Gq/R^5$;
- induction terms: $R^{-4},R^{-3},R^{-2}$;
- wave curvature: $\mathcal E\sim-Gq^{(4)}/(c^4R)$.

## Exact local strong-history efficiency for this geometry

For this plus quadrupole, the clean coherent outgoing-graviton record spectrum is

$$
S_G(\omega)\simeq\frac{2G}{5\hbar c^5}\omega^5
$$

in the adopted two-sided convention.

Define

$$
\nu_G=\frac{G\mu_BL_B^2Q_B\omega_B^3}{c^5}.
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal C_{\rm hist}^{(G)}(\epsilon)
=\frac54\nu_G\frac{|P(\epsilon)|^2}{\epsilon^{10}}.
}
$$

Limits:

$$
\epsilon\ll1:
\quad
\mathcal C\simeq\frac{45}{4}\nu_G\epsilon^{-10},
$$

$$
\epsilon\gg1:
\quad
\mathcal C\simeq\frac54\nu_G\epsilon^{-2},
$$

and at $\epsilon=1$,

$$
\boxed{\mathcal C(1)=\frac52\nu_G.}
$$

For laboratory systems $\nu_G\ll1$, so a single local differential receiver is extremely inefficient in the strong-history sense exactly where retardation becomes dynamically order unity.

This is a sufficient-witness/local-mode-capture limitation, **not** a no-go theorem for gravity-mediated entanglement.
