# 2. Encoder with thermal source bath

Use the RWA encoder

$$
H_{\rm enc}
=\hbar g\sigma_z(a^\dagger c+a c^\dagger),
$$

where

- $a$ is the finite-spoke source mode;
- $c$ is the work/controller mode.

Let the source mode have total damping

$$
\kappa_A
=\kappa_{g,A}+\sum_j\kappa_j,
$$

and source thermal injection rate

$$
\boxed{
\Gamma_{{\rm th},A}
=\sum_j\kappa_j\bar n_j.
}
$$

The gravitational input is taken as vacuum in the canonical benchmark.

If the controller also has a weak thermal environment, define

$$
\boxed{
\Gamma_{{\rm th},C}
=\kappa_C\bar n_C.
}
$$

Use the combined encoder thermal injection scale

$$
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm enc}
=\Gamma_{{\rm th},A}
+\Gamma_{{\rm th},C}.
}
$$

# 3. Total-excitation balance

The resonant beam-splitter interaction conserves total source+controller excitation:

$$
\boxed{
[H_{\rm enc},a^\dagger a+c^\dagger c]=0.
}
$$

Therefore the interaction itself does not create thermal quanta.

Let

$$
N=a^\dagger a+c^\dagger c.
$$

Subtract the deterministic coherent branch trajectory and consider only the covariance/excess-excitation contribution generated by the thermal baths.

For standard Markov thermal damping,

$$
\frac{d}{dt}\langle\delta N\rangle
=
-\kappa_A\langle\delta n_a\rangle
-\kappa_C\langle\delta n_c\rangle
+\Gamma_{{\rm th},A}
+\Gamma_{{\rm th},C}.
$$

Since the damping terms are nonpositive,

$$
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dt}\langle\delta N\rangle
\le
\Gamma_{\rm enc}.
}
$$

Starting from source/controller states with no excess thermal covariance relative to the intended coherent preparation,

$$
\boxed{
\langle\delta N(T_*)\rangle
\le
\Gamma_{\rm enc}T_*.
}
$$

This bound does not depend on the detailed coherent swap trajectory.

# 6. Gravitational fraction of encoder-injected thermal energy

At every time the source mode loses excitation into its physical ports with rates

$$
\kappa_{g,A},\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\ldots.
$$

The instantaneous fraction of source dissipation entering the gravitational port is

$$
\boxed{
\eta_g
=\frac{\kappa_{g,A}}{\kappa_A}.
}
$$

Any thermal excitation injected during the encoder can

- remain temporarily in the source;
- swap into the controller;
- return to the source;
- leave through a source output port;
- or be lost through a controller bath.

The total gravitationally emitted thermal energy attributable specifically to encoder-time thermal injections cannot exceed the fraction $\eta_g$ of the total injected quanta that subsequently dissipate through the source.

Therefore the conservative bound is

$$
\boxed{
\delta N_{g,\rm th}^{\rm enc}
\le
\eta_g\Gamma_{\rm enc}T_*.
}
$$

A single normalized gravitational output mode cannot contain more thermal occupation than the total thermal graviton number emitted into all gravitational modes, so

$$
\boxed{
\delta m_A^{\rm enc}
\le
\eta_g\Gamma_{\rm enc}T_*.
}
$$

This is deliberately a bound rather than an equality; detailed mode matching can only reduce the encoder-specific contribution.
