# Phase-Sensitive Gaussian Reduction

**Timestamp:** 2026-08-07 17:25 EDT  
**Status:** Structural extension using canonical Gaussian-unitary equivalence; closes deterministic pre/post-squeezing as a quantum-capability loophole for the covered channel class.

## 1. Question

Could a deterministic phase-sensitive receiver—e.g. a squeezer or degenerate parametric amplifier—beat the phase-insensitive causal-front threshold by amplifying only the branch-sensitive quadrature and avoiding the usual phase-insensitive half-quantum penalty?

There are two distinct possibilities:

1. **Gaussian-unitary preprocessing/postprocessing around a noisy canonical channel.**
2. **A genuinely different noisy phase-sensitive channel whose canonical invariants change.**

The first case can be resolved exactly.

---

## 2. Canonical-unitary form

Consider a one-mode Gaussian receiver channel that can be written

$$
\boxed{
\Psi
=\mathcal U_{\rm out}
\circ\Phi_{\tau,m}
\circ\mathcal U_{\rm in},
}
$$

where

- $\mathcal U_{\rm in}$ and $\mathcal U_{\rm out}$ are deterministic Gaussian unitaries generated by phase shifts/squeezing;
- $\Phi_{\tau,m}$ is the gauge-covariant phase-insensitive canonical channel used in `PHASE_INSENSITIVE_GAUSSIAN_BINARY_PROBE_THEOREM.md`.

One-mode Gaussian-channel classification is naturally organized up to such canonical unitary equivalence.

---

## 3. Entanglement-breaking invariance

A channel $\Phi$ is entanglement breaking iff

$$
(I\otimes\Phi)(\rho)
$$

is separable for every input bipartite state $\rho$.

Pre/post composition by reversible local unitaries cannot change this property. Therefore

$$
\boxed{
\Psi\text{ is EB}
\iff
\Phi_{\tau,m}\text{ is EB}.
}
$$

For the canonical channel,

$$
\Phi_{\tau,m}\text{ EB}
\iff
m\ge\tau.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{
\Psi\text{ non-EB}
\iff
m<\tau.
}
$$

No deterministic squeezer placed before or after a fixed canonical noisy channel can move this fundamental boundary.

---

## 4. Complete binary pure-Gaussian probe

The phase-insensitive theorem says that every nontrivial binary coherent hybrid input

$$
\sqrt p|0\rangle|\alpha\rangle
+e^{i\phi}\sqrt{1-p}|1\rangle|\beta\rangle
$$

is NPT after $\Phi_{\tau,m}$ iff $m<\tau$.

For $\Psi$, choose instead

$$
\boxed{
|\Psi_{\rm probe}\rangle
=\sqrt p|0\rangle
U_{\rm in}^\dagger|\alpha\rangle
+e^{i\phi}\sqrt{1-p}|1\rangle
U_{\rm in}^\dagger|\beta\rangle.
}
$$

If $U_{\rm in}$ contains squeezing, these are two displaced squeezed pure Gaussian states with the **same covariance** and different first moments.

After the input unitary,

$$
U_{\rm in}
U_{\rm in}^\dagger|\alpha\rangle
=|\alpha\rangle,
$$

so the canonical binary coherent theorem applies. The output unitary $U_{\rm out}$ is local and preserves NPT/separability.

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
(I\otimes\Psi)(|\Psi_{\rm probe}\rangle\langle\Psi_{\rm probe}|)
\text{ is NPT}
\iff
m<\tau.
}
$$

Thus the binary coherent completeness theorem extends, under Gaussian-unitary equivalence, to a **binary equal-covariance pure-Gaussian probe theorem**.

---

## 5. What phase-sensitive amplification can still do

Although deterministic squeezing cannot move the EB boundary of a fixed channel, it can change practical quantities:

- rotate the branch-sensitive quadrature into the receiver's low-noise axis;
- change the coherent displacement measured in laboratory units;
- reshape source–receiver temporal/spectral mode overlap;
- redistribute finite-certification signal between quadratures;
- reduce measurement cost for a chosen witness.

These can move a **finite experimental certification front** if the measurement is constrained, even though they do not move the underlying exact NPT boundary of the total channel.

## 6. Gravity interpretation

The incoming linearized-gravity branch-difference mode is naturally a coherent bosonic field mode. Suppose the receiver applies a deterministic phase-sensitive Gaussian unitary before a fixed noisy capture stage.

Then one may equivalently view the incoming gravitational binary coherent states as being mapped into a pair of squeezed coherent states before the canonical noisy stage.

The key quantum-capability question remains the canonical invariant

$$
\boxed{m<\tau.}
$$

Therefore

> **deterministic phase-sensitive gain can make one gravitational quadrature easier to measure, but cannot rescue a receiver whose overall Gaussian channel is entanglement breaking.**

This closes the simplest phase-sensitive loophole in the receiver argument.

---

## 7. What remains open

The reduction does **not** automatically cover:

- singular one-mode Gaussian canonical classes;
- channels with genuinely different phase-sensitive noise invariants not represented merely by reversible pre/post Gaussian unitaries around the canonical family;
- non-deterministic noiseless linear amplification;
- heralded filters;
- non-Gaussian receivers;
- unstable above-threshold parametric devices;
- phase-sensitive measurement/feedback with conditional postselection.

These remain possible receiver loopholes, but they are no longer ordinary deterministic squeezing loopholes.

## 8. Next implication

The natural broader statement to investigate is:

> For every nonsingular one-mode Gaussian channel that is Gaussian-unitarily equivalent to a gauge-covariant canonical channel, does a two-branch equal-covariance pure-Gaussian hybrid probe characterize its EB boundary exactly?

Within the explicit decomposition above the answer is yes by construction. The remaining task is to map precisely which Holevo canonical classes are covered and avoid claiming the result for singular classes without separate proof.
