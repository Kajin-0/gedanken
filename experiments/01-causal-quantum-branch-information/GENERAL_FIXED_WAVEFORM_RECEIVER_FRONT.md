# General Fixed-Waveform Receiver Front

**Timestamp:** 2026-08-07 19:58 EDT  
**Status:** Major correction/generalization. This supersedes any wording that treats the earlier logarithmic front as universal. The logarithmic formula is recovered only under additional initial-state and waveform-optimization assumptions.

## 1. Why the correction is necessary

The earlier receiver front used

$$
\tau_{m opt}(t)
=\frac{\kappa_\Delta}{\kappa}
(1-e^{-\kappa t})
$$

and compared it with a time-independent thermal occupation

$$
m_*=\Gamma_{\rm th}/\kappa.
$$

That corresponds to two special choices:

1. the receiver begins in its stationary thermal state,
   $$
   n_0=m_*;
   $$
2. the incoming temporal mode is optimized for the chosen observation time so that the Cauchy–Schwarz loading bound is saturated.

Neither assumption is universal.

A ground-state-cooled receiver has $n_0\simeq0$, and a real source produces one fixed waveform rather than a different waveform optimized separately for every observation time.

The correct general object is therefore the **fixed-waveform time-dependent Gaussian channel**.

---

## 2. Receiver Langevin equation

After the retarded signal arrives, write

$$
\dot c(t)
=-\frac{\kappa}{2}c(t)
+\sqrt{\kappa_\Delta}\,b_{\Delta}^{\rm in}(t)
+\sum_a\sqrt{\kappa_a}\,b_a^{\rm in}(t),
$$

where

$$
\kappa
=\kappa_\Delta+\sum_a\kappa_a
$$

includes all damping ports, including vacuum ports.

Define occupied-bath injection

$$
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm th}
=\sum_a\kappa_a\bar n_a.
}
$$

Let the receiver initial occupation be

$$
\boxed{
n_0=\langle c^\dagger(0)c(0)\rangle.
}
$$

No assumption of thermal stationarity is made.

## 3. Fixed incoming branch mode

Let the source produce one normalized incoming temporal branch-difference mode

$$
\boxed{
\int_0^\infty ds\,|f(s)|^2=1.
}
$$

Decompose the source input field as

$$
b_\Delta^{\rm in}(s)
=f(s)b_f+b_\perp(s),
$$

where $b_f$ is the selected normalized source mode and $b_\perp$ contains orthogonal modes.

The receiver solution is

$$
c(t)
=e^{-\kappa t/2}c(0)
+\sqrt{\kappa_\Delta}
\int_0^t ds\,e^{-\kappa(t-s)/2}f(s)b_f
+\text{environmental modes}.
$$

Thus the coherent intensity transmission from the selected source mode into the receiver at time $t$ is

$$
\boxed{
\tau_f(t)
=\kappa_\Delta
\left|
\int_0^t ds\,
e^{-\kappa(t-s)/2}f(s)
\right|^2.
}
$$

This is the physically relevant signal term for one fixed source waveform.

## 4. Exact receiver noise occupation

Set the selected source input mode to vacuum. The initial receiver occupation decays as

$$
n_0e^{-\kappa t}.
$$

Each occupied Markov bath contributes

$$
\kappa_a\bar n_a
\int_0^t ds\,e^{-\kappa(t-s)}.
$$

Therefore the mean output occupation generated by the receiver initial state and occupied baths is

$$
\boxed{
m(t)
=n_0e^{-\kappa t}
+\frac{\Gamma_{\rm th}}{\kappa}
(1-e^{-\kappa t}).
}
$$

Vacuum ports are already included in the complete-positivity noise floor but contribute zero occupation to $m(t)$ in the $\Phi_{\tau,m}$ convention.

The stationary occupation is

$$
\boxed{
n_{\rm ss}=\Gamma_{\rm th}/\kappa.
}
$$

## 5. General exact capability condition

At every fixed time the source-mode $\to$ receiver map is a gauge-covariant phase-insensitive Gaussian channel with parameters

$$
\tau=\tau_f(t),
\qquad
m=m(t).
$$

Hence the channel is non-entanglement-breaking exactly when

$$
\boxed{
\tau_f(t)>m(t).
}
$$

For the binary coherent hybrid source used in Experiment 01, the source-receiver state is NPT under the same condition.

Therefore the general fixed-waveform capability front is

$$
\boxed{
T_{\rm cap}[f]
=\inf\{t>0:\tau_f(t)>m(t)\}.
}
$$

Restoring propagation delay,

$$
\boxed{
T_{\rm cap}(R;f)
=\frac Rc+T_{\rm cap}[f_R],
}
$$

where $f_R$ is the actual source wavepacket arriving at distance $R$, including propagation/mode filtering.

This is the general receiver front. In most cases it must be found from the source waveform rather than from one universal closed logarithm.

## 6. Cauchy–Schwarz envelope

For any normalized waveform restricted to the interval $[0,t]$,

$$
\tau_f(t)
\le
\frac{\kappa_\Delta}{\kappa}
(1-e^{-\kappa t}).
$$

Equality is obtained by a waveform proportional to the time reverse of the receiver impulse response on that interval,

$$
f_t(s)
\propto
e^{-\kappa(t-s)/2}.
$$

Crucially, the saturating waveform depends on the target time $t$.

Therefore the function

$$
\tau_{\rm env}(t)
=\frac{\kappa_\Delta}{\kappa}
(1-e^{-\kappa t})
$$

is a **protocol-optimized envelope**, not generally the trajectory of one fixed physical source wavepacket.

---

## 7. Optimized envelope with arbitrary initial occupation

Compare the envelope to the exact noise occupation:

$$
\frac{\kappa_\Delta}{\kappa}(1-e^{-\kappa t})
>
n_0e^{-\kappa t}
+\frac{\Gamma_{\rm th}}{\kappa}(1-e^{-\kappa t}).
$$

A non-EB window is possible only if

$$
\boxed{
\kappa_\Delta>\Gamma_{\rm th}.
}
$$

When this holds, the earliest protocol-optimized envelope crossing is

$$
\boxed{
T_{\rm cap}^{\rm env}
=
\frac1\kappa
\ln\left[
1+
\frac{\kappa n_0}
{\kappa_\Delta-\Gamma_{\rm th}}
\right].
}
$$

This is the corrected general form of the earlier logarithmic result.

## 8. Stationary thermal receiver recovers the old logarithm

If the receiver is initially in equilibrium with its occupied baths,

$$
n_0=n_{\rm ss}
=\Gamma_{\rm th}/\kappa,
$$

then

$$
T_{\rm cap}^{\rm env}
=
\frac1\kappa
\ln\left[
1+
\frac{\Gamma_{\rm th}}
{\kappa_\Delta-\Gamma_{\rm th}}
\right],
$$

or

$$
\boxed{
T_{\rm cap}^{\rm env}
=
\frac1\kappa
\ln\frac{\kappa_\Delta}
{\kappa_\Delta-\Gamma_{\rm th}}.
}
$$

This is exactly the logarithmic front previously derived.

Therefore the old expression is **correct but special**:

$$
\boxed{
\text{stationary initial receiver}
+\text{time-targeted optimal waveform}.
}
$$

It must not be presented as the universal gravitational receiver front.

## 9. Ground-state receiver changes the capability onset qualitatively

If the receiver is prepared in its ground state,

$$
n_0=0,
$$

then the optimized-envelope crossing becomes

$$
\boxed{
T_{\rm cap}^{\rm env}=0
}
$$

whenever

$$
\kappa_\Delta>\Gamma_{\rm th}.
$$

This does **not** mean finite entanglement can be certified instantaneously.

It means only that, within an unconstrained Markov temporal-mode optimization, arbitrarily small nonzero capture and proportionally small thermal noise can define a non-EB channel immediately after causal contact.

The amount of transmitted entanglement vanishes as $t\to0^+$.

A fixed finite-bandwidth source waveform generally restores a finite capability onset because its capture begins quadratically in time while bath noise begins linearly.

## 10. Short-time behavior for a fixed regular waveform

Suppose $f(s)$ is finite at the wavefront,

$$
f(0)\neq\infty.
$$

At short time,

$$
\int_0^t ds\,
e^{-\kappa(t-s)/2}f(s)
=f(0)t+O(t^2),
$$

so

$$
\boxed{
\tau_f(t)
=\kappa_\Delta|f(0)|^2t^2+O(t^3).
}
$$

For a ground-state receiver,

$$
\boxed{
m(t)=\Gamma_{\rm th}t+O(t^2).
}
$$

Thus sufficiently near the wavefront,

$$
m(t)\gg\tau_f(t)
$$

for every nonzero $\Gamma_{\rm th}$.

The fixed-waveform channel is initially EB and can cross into the non-EB region only later.

The leading estimate is

$$
\boxed{
T_{\rm cap}[f]
\sim
\frac{\Gamma_{\rm th}}
{\kappa_\Delta|f(0)|^2}
}
$$

when the crossing occurs within the short-time regime.

This is qualitatively analogous to a discrete source–receiver beam-splitter transfer, for which transfer probability also begins as $t^2$.

## 11. Independent check from Toccacelo et al. (2026)

Toccacelo, Beitel, Andersen, and Pikovski model a passing quantized gravitational-wave mode $a$ resonantly interacting with a bulk acoustic detector mode $b$ through

$$
H_{\rm int}
=\hbar\gamma_g(b^\dagger a+ba^\dagger).
$$

With detector damping $\kappa$ and thermal environment occupation $\bar N$, their Appendix C gives, for a detector initially in its ground state,

$$
\sigma_{\rm bar}(t)
=e^{-\kappa t}
\left[
\cos^2(\gamma_gt)\sigma_{\rm bar}(0)
+\sin^2(\gamma_gt)\sigma_{\rm grav}(0)
\right]
+(1-e^{-\kappa t})(\bar N+1/2)I.
$$

Therefore their already-arrived GW-mode $\to$ detector reduced map is a phase-insensitive Gaussian channel with

$$
\boxed{
\tau_{\rm TBAP}(t)
=e^{-\kappa t}\sin^2(\gamma_gt),
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
m_{\rm TBAP}(t)
=(1-e^{-\kappa t})\bar N.
}
$$

This channel identification is an inference from their published covariance evolution; their paper does not formulate the result as an EB crossing.

Applying the standard EB boundary gives

$$
\boxed{
e^{-\kappa t}\sin^2(\gamma_gt)
>
(1-e^{-\kappa t})\bar N
}
$$

for a non-EB gravitational-wave-to-detector channel.

At short time,

$$
\tau\simeq\gamma_g^2t^2,
\qquad
m\simeq\kappa\bar Nt,
$$

which displays precisely the $t^2$ signal versus $t$ thermal-noise competition described above.

This is a strong independent validation of the fixed-waveform/discrete-mode logic and a major prior-art constraint on broad novelty claims.

## 12. Relation to Miki–Li–Chen (2026)

Miki, Li, and Chen study two Newtonian gravitationally coupled mechanical oscillators with thermal damping.

Their full two-mode channel has an EB condition

$$
\boxed{
2N_{\rm th}\tanh(\gamma_mt_G)\ge1,
}
$$

which is independent of gravitational coupling because the gravitational beam-splitter is a unitary part of the complete two-mode channel.

In the weak-damping limit,

$$
2\gamma_mt_GN_{\rm th}\ge1.
$$

This is a **late-time EB degradation** condition for the full two-mode channel.

By contrast, the one-way reduced transfer map $A_{\rm in}\to B_{\rm out}$ inferred from their input-output coefficients has signal transmission

$$
\tau_{A\to B}(t)
=e^{-2\gamma_mt}\sin^2(g_Gt),
$$

and a thermal-noise term that grows from zero. It therefore exhibits the same qualitative early EB $\to$ possible non-EB transfer behavior as the Toccacelo gravitational-wave detector map.

This reduced-channel observation is an inference, not a result explicitly stated by Miki et al.
