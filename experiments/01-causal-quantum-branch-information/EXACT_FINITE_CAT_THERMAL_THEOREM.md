# Exact Finite-Cat Thermal-Attenuator Theorem

**Updated:** 2026-08-07 16:46 EDT  
**Status:** Analytic result within the single-mode phase-insensitive thermal-attenuator model; novelty not yet established.

## 1. Setup

Consider a source path qubit coherently correlated with one bosonic branch-difference mode,

$$
|\Psi_a\rangle
=
\frac{|L\rangle|a\rangle+|R\rangle|-a\rangle}{\sqrt2},
\qquad a\neq0.
$$

Without loss of generality take $a>0$ real, since the thermal attenuator is phase covariant.

Send the bosonic mode through a thermal attenuator of transmissivity $\eta$ whose environmental mode has mean occupation $\bar n$. Define the thermal occupation added to the receiver when the signal input is vacuum,

$$
\boxed{m=(1-\eta)\bar n.}
$$

The standard entanglement-breaking boundary is

$$
\boxed{
\eta\le m
\iff
\eta\le\frac{\bar n}{\bar n+1}.
}
$$

The question is stronger than the channel-level statement: does this **specific finite coherent-cat family** remain NPT for every $a\neq0$ whenever the channel is not entanglement breaking?

## 2. Theorem

> **Finite-cat thermal theorem.** For every finite $a\neq0$, the source-receiver output state is NPT if and only if the thermal attenuator is not entanglement breaking:
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> \rho_{AB}^{\Gamma_A}\not\succeq0
> \iff
> \eta>m
> \iff
> \eta>\frac{\bar n}{\bar n+1}.
> }
> $$
>
> The sign boundary is independent of cat amplitude. Cat amplitude controls the amount of transferred entanglement, but not whether entanglement is present.

The vacuum case $m=0$ is the continuous limiting case: every $\eta>0$ preserves some NPT entanglement for every finite $a\neq0$.

## 3. Output block structure

Write

$$
\rho_{AB}
=
\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho_+ & X\\
X^\dagger & \rho_-
\end{pmatrix},
$$

where

$$
\rho_\pm=\mathcal E_{\eta,\bar n}(|\pm a\rangle\langle\pm a|),
$$

and

$$
X=\mathcal E_{\eta,\bar n}(|a\rangle\langle-a|).
$$

Let

$$
b=\sqrt\eta\,a,
\qquad
r=\frac{m}{m+1},
\qquad
s=\frac{b}{m+1}.
$$

The Gaussian characteristic-function action gives the Bargmann/normal-ordered blocks

$$
\boxed{
\rho_+
=P\,e^{s a^\dagger}r^{\hat N}e^{s a},
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\rho_-
=P\,e^{-s a^\dagger}r^{\hat N}e^{-s a},
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
X
=C\,e^{s a^\dagger}r^{\hat N}e^{-s a},
}
$$

with

$$
P
=
\frac{1}{m+1}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{\eta a^2}{m+1}\right],
$$

$$
C
=
\frac{1}{m+1}
\exp\!\left[-2a^2+\frac{\eta a^2}{m+1}\right].
$$

## 4. Factorization

Define

$$
L_+
=\sqrt P\,e^{s a^\dagger}r^{\hat N/2},
\qquad
L_-
=\sqrt P\,e^{-s a^\dagger}r^{\hat N/2}.
$$

Then

$$
\rho_+=L_+L_+^\dagger,
\qquad
\rho_-=L_-L_-^\dagger,
$$

and

$$
X=tL_+L_-^\dagger,
$$

where

$$
\boxed{
t=\frac CP
=\exp\!\left[-2a^2+\frac{2\eta a^2}{m+1}\right].
}
$$

After partial transpose on the source,

$$
\rho_{AB}^{\Gamma_A}
=
\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho_+ & X^\dagger\\
X & \rho_-
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

On the standard analytic domain, apply the block-diagonal congruence generated by $L_+^{-1}$ and $L_-^{-1}$. The diagonal blocks become identity. The upper-right block is

$$
K
=tL_+^{-1}L_-L_+^\dagger(L_-^{-1})^\dagger.
$$

Using

$$
r^{-\hat N/2}a^\dagger r^{\hat N/2}
=r^{-1/2}a^\dagger,
$$

one obtains

$$
K=t\,e^{-u a^\dagger}e^{u a},
\qquad
\boxed{u=\frac{2s}{\sqrt r}}.
$$

Since

$$
e^{-u a^\dagger}e^{u a}
=e^{u^2/2}D(-u),
$$

where $D(-u)$ is unitary,

$$
\boxed{K=qD(-u)}
$$

with

$$
q=te^{u^2/2}.
$$

Substitution gives

$$
\boxed{
q
=\exp\!\left[
\frac{2a^2}{m}(\eta-m)
\right].
}
$$

Thus formally the partial transpose is reduced to

$$
\boxed{
\rho'^{\Gamma_A}
=
\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}
I & qD(-u)\\
qD(u) & I
\end{pmatrix}.
}
$$

## 5. Domain-safe explicit negative vector

The inverses $L_\pm^{-1}$ are unbounded, so the NPT proof should not rely only on a formal statement that the congruence preserves the full spectrum. Instead construct one explicit normalizable negative direction.

Take

$$
|\phi\rangle=|0\rangle
$$

and define in the congruent representation

$$
|y\rangle
=
\begin{pmatrix}
|0\rangle\\
-D(u)|0\rangle
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{
\langle y|\rho'^{\Gamma_A}|y\rangle
=1-q.
}
$$

The corresponding vector in the original source-receiver Hilbert space is

$$
|x\rangle
=
G^\dagger|y\rangle,
\qquad
G=\operatorname{diag}(L_+^{-1},L_-^{-1}).
$$

The first component is simply

$$
(L_+^{-1})^\dagger|0\rangle=P^{-1/2}|0\rangle.
$$

For the second component, $D(u)|0\rangle=|u\rangle$ and

$$
(L_-^{-1})^\dagger|u\rangle
=P^{-1/2}e^{s a}r^{-\hat N/2}|u\rangle.
$$

Because $m>0$ implies $0<r<1$, this is a finite coherent vector. Explicitly,

$$
r^{-\hat N/2}|u\rangle
=\exp\!\left[-\frac{u^2}{2}+\frac{u^2}{2r}\right]
\left|\frac{u}{\sqrt r}\right\rangle,
$$

and therefore the original negative test vector is normalizable for every finite $a$ and every $m>0$.

Hence

$$
\langle x|\rho_{AB}^{\Gamma_A}|x\rangle
=1-q.
$$

If

$$
q>1,
$$

this expectation value is strictly negative. Since

$$
q>1
\iff
\eta>m,
$$

we have the fully explicit implication

$$
\boxed{
\eta>m
\Rightarrow
\rho_{AB}\text{ is NPT}
}
$$

for every finite $a\neq0$.

This avoids relying on spectral equivalence under an unbounded inverse congruence.

## 6. PPT/separable direction

For

$$
\eta\le m,
$$

the thermal attenuator is entanglement breaking. It therefore destroys source-mode entanglement for every input state, including this finite-cat family. Hence

$$
\boxed{
\eta\le m
\Rightarrow
\rho_{AB}\text{ is separable and PPT}.
}
$$

Combining the two directions proves the theorem for $m>0$.

For $m=0$, the channel is pure loss. Its environmental branch overlap is finite for every finite $a$, and every $\eta>0$ transfers nonzero source-receiver entanglement; only $\eta=0$ is entanglement breaking.

## 7. Consequence for the causal receiver

At fixed time $t$, a passive linear memory driven by one matched gravitational difference mode and branch-independent Gaussian thermal baths defines an effective phase-insensitive thermal attenuator from the incoming difference mode to the stored receiver mode.

Let

$$
\eta_f(t)
$$

be the coherent transfer coefficient and

$$
m(t)
$$

be the branch-independent receiver thermal occupation. Then for **every finite source cat**,

$$
\boxed{
\rho_{AB}(t)\text{ is NPT}
\iff
\eta_f(t)>m(t).
}
$$

Thus

$$
T_{\rm NPT}=\inf\{t:\eta_f(t)>m(t)\}
$$

is amplitude independent at the level of the exact sign boundary.

## 8. Consequence for the waveform-independent front bound

For

$$
\dot c
=-\frac{\kappa_{\rm tot}}2c
+\sqrt{\kappa_\Delta}\,b_{\rm in}
+\sum_a\sqrt{\kappa_a}\,\xi_a,
$$

any normalized incoming waveform obeys

$$
\eta_f(\tau)
\le
\frac{\kappa_\Delta}{\kappa_{\rm tot}}
\left(1-e^{-\kappa_{\rm tot}\tau}\right).
$$

For a stationary thermal receiver,

$$
m_*=\frac{\Gamma_{\rm th}}{\kappa_{\rm tot}},
\qquad
\Gamma_{\rm th}=\sum_a\bar n_a\kappa_a.
$$

Therefore the exact finite-cat front satisfies

$$
\boxed{
T_{\rm NPT}
\ge
\frac Rc
+
\frac1{\kappa_{\rm tot}}
\ln\!\left(
\frac{\kappa_\Delta}
{\kappa_\Delta-\Gamma_{\rm th}}
\right)

$$

when

$$
\kappa_\Delta>\Gamma_{\rm th}.
$$

If

$$
\kappa_\Delta\le\Gamma_{\rm th},
$$

no finite coherent cat can produce an NPT source-receiver state through this receiver channel.

The matched time-reversed ringdown waveform saturates the capture inequality, so the front bound is tight within the model.

## 9. Interpretation

The theorem separates channel quality from signal size:

$$
\boxed{
\text{channel quantum-capable}
\iff
\eta_f>m.
}
$$

Making the source cat larger cannot open a thermally entanglement-breaking channel. Conversely, once the channel is non-entanglement-breaking, every finite nonzero cat transmits some NPT entanglement.
