# Exact Finite-Cat Thermal-Attenuator Theorem

**Timestamp:** 2026-08-07 16:46 EDT  
**Status:** Analytic result within the single-mode phase-insensitive thermal-attenuator model; novelty not yet established.

## 1. Setup

Consider a source path qubit coherently correlated with one bosonic branch-difference mode,

$$
|\Psi_a\rangle
=
\frac{|L\rangle|a\rangle+|R\rangle|-a\rangle}{\sqrt2},
\qquad a\neq0.
$$

Without loss of generality take $a>0$ real, since the thermal attenuator is phase covariant.

Send the bosonic mode through a thermal attenuator of transmissivity $\eta$ whose environmental mode has mean occupation $\bar n$. Define the thermal occupation added to the receiver when the signal input is vacuum,

$$
\boxed{m=(1-\eta)\bar n.}
$$

The standard entanglement-breaking boundary of the thermal attenuator is

$$
\boxed{
\eta\le m
\iff
\eta\le\frac{\bar n}{\bar n+1}.
}
$$

The question is stronger than the channel-level statement: does the specific finite-amplitude source-cat family remain NPT for every $a\neq0$ whenever the channel is not entanglement breaking?

## 2. Theorem

> **Finite-cat thermal theorem.** For every finite $a\neq0$, the source-receiver output state is NPT if and only if the thermal attenuator is not entanglement breaking:
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> \rho_{AB}^{\Gamma_A}\not\succeq0
> \iff
> \eta>m
> \iff
> \eta>\frac{\bar n}{\bar n+1}.
> }
> $$
>
> Thus the sign boundary is independent of cat amplitude. The cat amplitude controls the amount of transferred entanglement, but not whether entanglement is present.

The vacuum case $m=0$ is the continuous limiting case: every $\eta>0$ preserves some NPT entanglement for every finite $a\neq0$.

## 3. Output block structure

Write the output in the source path basis as

$$
\rho_{AB}
=
\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho_+ & X\\
X^\dagger & \rho_-
\end{pmatrix},
$$

where

$$
\rho_\pm=\mathcal E_{\eta,\bar n}(|\pm a\rangle\langle\pm a|),
$$

and

$$
X=\mathcal E_{\eta,\bar n}(|a\rangle\langle-a|).
$$

Let

$$
b=\sqrt\eta\,a,
\qquad
r=\frac{m}{m+1},
\qquad
s=\frac{b}{m+1}.
$$

In Bargmann/normal-ordered form the three bosonic blocks are

$$
\boxed{
\rho_+
=P\,e^{s a^\dagger}r^{\hat N}e^{s a},
}
$$

$$
\boxed{
\rho_-
=P\,e^{-s a^\dagger}r^{\hat N}e^{-s a},
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
X
=C\,e^{s a^\dagger}r^{\hat N}e^{-s a},
}
$$

with

$$
P
=
\frac{1}{m+1}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{\eta a^2}{m+1}\right],
$$

$$
C
=
\frac{1}{m+1}
\exp\!\left[-2a^2+\frac{\eta a^2}{m+1}\right].
$$

These expressions follow directly from the Gaussian characteristic-function action of the thermal attenuator.

## 4. Factorization

Define

$$
L_+
=\sqrt P\,e^{s a^\dagger}r^{\hat N/2},
\qquad
L_-
=\sqrt P\,e^{-s a^\dagger}r^{\hat N/2}.
$$

Then

$$
\rho_+=L_+L_+^\dagger,
\qquad
\rho_-=L_-L_-^\dagger,
$$

and

$$
X=tL_+L_-^\dagger,
$$

where

$$
\boxed{
t=\frac CP
=\exp\!\left[-2a^2+\frac{2\eta a^2}{m+1}\right].
}
$$

After partial transpose on the source,

$$
\rho_{AB}^{\Gamma_A}
=
\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}
\rho_+ & X^\dagger\\
X & \rho_-
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Apply the block-diagonal congruence generated by $L_+^{-1}$ and $L_-^{-1}$ on the natural analytic domain. The diagonal blocks become identity operators. The off-diagonal block reduces to

$$
K
=
tL_+^{-1}L_-L_+^\dagger(L_-^{-1})^\dagger.
$$

Using

$$
r^{-\hat N/2}a^\dagger r^{\hat N/2}
=r^{-1/2}a^\dagger,
$$

one obtains

$$
K=t\,e^{-u a^\dagger}e^{u a},
\qquad
u=\frac{2s}{\sqrt r}.
$$

The Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff identity gives

$$
e^{-u a^\dagger}e^{u a}
=e^{u^2/2}D(-u),
$$

where $D(-u)$ is unitary. Therefore

$$
\boxed{K=qD(-u)}
$$

with

$$
q=te^{u^2/2}.
$$

Substituting $r,s,t$ gives the decisive simplification

$$
\boxed{
q
=\exp\!\left[
\frac{2a^2}{m}(\eta-m)
\right].
}
$$

Thus the partial transpose is congruent, on the relevant domain, to

$$
\boxed{
\frac12
\begin{pmatrix}
I & qD(-u)\\
qD(u) & I
\end{pmatrix}.
}
$$

## 5. NPT direction

Because $D(u)$ is unitary, choose any normalized analytic vector $|\phi\rangle$ and form

$$
|y\rangle
=
\begin{pmatrix}
|\phi\rangle\\
-D(u)|\phi\rangle
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

For the congruent operator,

$$
\langle y|\rho'^{\Gamma_A}|y\rangle
=(1-q)\langle\phi|\phi\rangle.
$$

Hence if

$$
q>1,
$$

the partial transpose has a negative direction. Since

$$
q>1
\iff
\eta>m,
$$

we obtain

$$
\boxed{
\eta>m
\Rightarrow
\rho_{AB}\text{ is NPT}
}
$$

for every finite $a\neq0$.

One may take $|\phi\rangle=|0\rangle$; the corresponding preimage under the congruence is normalizable for $m>0$, so the negative direction is not merely formal.

## 6. PPT/separable direction

For

$$
\eta\le m,
$$

the thermal attenuator is entanglement breaking. Therefore it destroys source-mode entanglement for every input state, including the finite-cat family above. Hence

$$
\boxed{
\eta\le m
\Rightarrow
\rho_{AB}\text{ is separable and therefore PPT}.
}
$$

Combining both directions proves the theorem.

## 7. Consequence for the causal receiver

At a fixed receiver time $t$, a passive linear memory driven by one matched gravitational difference mode and branch-independent Gaussian thermal baths defines an effective phase-insensitive thermal attenuator from that incoming mode to the stored receiver mode.

Let

$$
\eta_f(t)
$$

be the coherent transfer coefficient of the branch-difference mode and let

$$
m(t)
$$

be the receiver's branch-independent thermal occupation at that time.

Then for **every finite source cat**,

$$
\boxed{
\rho_{AB}(t)\text{ is NPT}
\iff
\eta_f(t)>m(t).
}
$$

This upgrades the earlier weak-cat result to an exact finite-cat statement within the thermal single-mode receiver model.

Therefore the causal NPT front

$$
T_{\rm NPT}
=\inf\{t:\eta_f(t)>m(t)\}
$$

is independent of cat amplitude $N_\Delta$ at the level of its sign boundary.

Cat amplitude controls the magnitude and experimental visibility of the negativity, but it does not move the exact entanglement-onset boundary.

## 8. Consequence for the waveform-independent front bound

For the linear receiver

$$
\dot c
=-\frac{\kappa_{\rm tot}}2c
+\sqrt{\kappa_\Delta}\,b_{\rm in}
+\sum_a\sqrt{\kappa_a}\,\xi_a,
$$

Cauchy-Schwarz gives, for any normalized incoming mode waveform,

$$
\eta_f(\tau)
\le
\frac{\kappa_\Delta}{\kappa_{\rm tot}}
\left(1-e^{-\kappa_{\rm tot}\tau}\right).
$$

For a stationary thermal receiver,

$$
m_*=\frac{\Gamma_{\rm th}}{\kappa_{\rm tot}},
\qquad
\Gamma_{\rm th}=\sum_a\bar n_a\kappa_a.
$$

The exact finite-cat theorem therefore makes the previously derived causal speed limit exact for the whole finite-cat family:

$$
\boxed{
T_{\rm NPT}
\ge
\frac Rc
+
\frac1{\kappa_{\rm tot}}
\ln\!\left(
\frac{\kappa_\Delta}
{\kappa_\Delta-\Gamma_{\rm th}}
\right)
}
$$

whenever

$$
\kappa_\Delta>\Gamma_{\rm th}.
$$

If

$$
\kappa_\Delta\le\Gamma_{\rm th},
$$

no finite cat can produce an NPT source-receiver state through this receiver channel.

The matched time-reversed ringdown waveform saturates the capture inequality, so the front bound is tight within the model.

## 9. Why this matters for Experiment 01

The previous logic separated three issues:

1. when a branch-dependent gravitational signal can arrive;
2. when the receiver channel is capable of carrying entanglement;
3. how much source-receiver entanglement is generated.

This theorem now shows that, for the full finite-cat family in the thermal single-mode model, issue (2) has an exact amplitude-independent boundary:

$$
\boxed{
\text{quantum branch channel open}
\iff
\eta_f>m.
}
$$

Making the source cat larger cannot open a thermally entanglement-breaking channel. Conversely, once the channel is non-entanglement-breaking, every finite nonzero cat carries some NPT entanglement through it.

This is **not yet a novelty claim**. A broader literature search and independent proof check are required before presenting it as new.
