# 2. Strongest new collision — Matsui 2026

Hiroki Matsui, **“Graviton-induced which-path decoherence in matter-wave interferometry,”** arXiv:2607.20867 (submitted 23 July 2026).

This is the closest source-side collision found so far.

Matsui considers two semiclassical matter branches described by total stress tensors

$$
T_b^{\mu\nu}(x),
$$

with branch difference

$$
\Delta T^{\mu\nu}=T_1^{\mu\nu}-T_2^{\mu\nu}.
$$

Crucially, for the closed source used in the paper,

$$
\boxed{
\partial_\mu T_b^{\mu\nu}=0,
\qquad
\partial_\mu\Delta T^{\mu\nu}=0.
}
$$

The branches coincide before splitting and after recombination.

Because linearized gravity couples linearly to the c-number branch source, each branch drives a coherent displacement of the graviton modes. Matsui writes the branch-conditioned displacement amplitudes in the form

$$
\alpha_{b,s}(\mathbf k)
\propto
\epsilon^{s*}_{ij}(\hat{\mathbf k})
\widetilde T_b^{ij}(\omega_k,\mathbf k).
$$

The complete branch evolution is therefore a conditional multimode displacement.

Define

$$
\Delta\alpha=\alpha_1-\alpha_2.
$$

Matsui then defines

$$
\boxed{
N_\Delta
=\sum_s\int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}
|\Delta\alpha_s(\mathbf k)|^2.
}
$$

The paper explicitly identifies this as

- the mean graviton number in the auxiliary coherent field sourced by the difference stress tensor; and equivalently
- the squared phase-space distance between the two branch-conditioned radiation states.

For the graviton vacuum,

$$
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm vac}=\frac12N_\Delta.
}
$$

This is essentially the same physical interpretation used throughout Experiment 01 for the branch-difference graviton mode.

## Verdict

The following are **not novelty claims** for this repository:

- a closed conserved branch source producing branch-conditioned coherent graviton states;
- exact conditional displacement evolution for linearized gravitons;
- the use of a difference displacement $\Delta\alpha$;
- the identification of $N_\Delta=\|\Delta\alpha\|^2$ as branch-radiation distinguishability;
- the vacuum relation between graviton which-branch decoherence and $N_\Delta/2$;
- the statement that all radiative which-branch information is encoded by the difference-source displacement vector.

The repository may retain its one-normalized-difference-mode compression as notation and as a useful reduction for the receiver calculation, but should not present the underlying branch-difference radiation structure as newly discovered.

# 3. Laga–Suyama 2026 — coherent quantum radiation from a classical source

Felix Laga and Teruaki Suyama, **“Quantum description of gravitational waves generated by a classical source,”** arXiv:2604.20228 (2026).

They quantize the linearized gravitational-wave field while treating the matter energy-momentum tensor as a classical source.

Their main source-side results include:

1. the expectation value of the quantum gravitational-wave operator equals the standard **retarded classical solution**;
2. the far-zone field reduces to the standard quadrupole field;
3. the emitted graviton statistics have equal mean and variance, as expected for coherent radiation / Poisson emission.

Thus the broad statement

> a classical or semiclassical quadrupole source drives a coherent quantum gravitational wave whose mean follows the classical retarded field

is prior art.

## Verdict

Do not claim novelty for

- coherent-state gravitational radiation from a classical source;
- recovery of the retarded classical quadrupole waveform as the field expectation value;
- Poisson graviton counting from a classical source.

The repository's source contribution must instead be tied to its **explicit branch-controlled conserved elastic actuator architecture** and to the way that source is connected quantitatively to a receiver.

# 4. Toccacelo–Beitel–Andersen–Pikovski 2026 — quantum GW → resonant receiver

Kristian Toccacelo, Thomas Beitel, Ulrik Lund Andersen, and Igor Pikovski, **“Quantum State Characterization of Gravitational Waves via Graviton Counting Statistics,”** arXiv:2602.09125 (2026).

They consider a quantized incident gravitational-wave mode $a$ coupled to a collective acoustic detector mode $b$.

Under the rotating-wave approximation the interaction is a beam-splitter Hamiltonian,

$$
\boxed{
H_{\rm int}
=\hbar\gamma_g
(b^\dagger a+ba^\dagger)
}
$$

up to the interaction-picture phase convention.

On resonance,

$$
b(t)
=e^{-i\omega t}
\left[
\cos(\gamma_gt)b
-i\sin(\gamma_gt)a
\right].
$$

They explicitly treat general Gaussian gravitational-wave states and the transfer of their first and second moments into the detector.

They also add Markov thermal detector noise. Their Appendix C gives an open Gaussian receiver evolution of the form

$$
\sigma_b(t)
=e^{-\kappa t}
\left[
\cos^2(\gamma_gt)\sigma_b(0)
+\sin^2(\gamma_gt)\sigma_g(0)
\right]
+
(1-e^{-\kappa t})(\bar N+1/2)I,
$$

and

$$
\bar r_b(t)
=e^{-\kappa t/2}
\sin(\gamma_gt)\bar r_g(0).
$$

Thus, after fixing the detector input state and tracing the unused output, the incident-GW→detector map is already an explicit noisy phase-insensitive Gaussian transfer channel.

Applying the standard EB criterion to their published map gives, by inference,

$$
\tau(t)
=e^{-\kappa t}\sin^2(\gamma_gt),
$$

$$
m(t)
=(1-e^{-\kappa t})\bar N,
$$

and therefore the non-EB condition

$$
\boxed{
 e^{-\kappa t}\sin^2(\gamma_gt)
>
(1-e^{-\kappa t})\bar N.
}
$$

The EB interpretation is the repository's inference from their equations, not a claim made explicitly in their paper.

## Verdict

The following are **not novelty claims**:

- a quantized propagating GW mode coupled resonantly to a material quantum mode;
- beam-splitter-like graviton/phonon state transfer;
- transfer of Gaussian GW statistics into a resonant detector;
- thermal Markov noise in the receiver;
- the existence, in substance, of a finite noisy quantum-transfer window in such a receiver.

The repository remains different because it derives the **incident normalized gravitational mode from a controlled distant source and fixes its free-space capture normalization** rather than assuming an incident mode is already present at the detector.

# 5. Mari–Zippilli–Vitali — gravitational channels and EB thresholds

Andrea Mari, Stefano Zippilli, and David Vitali, **“Can gravity mediate the transmission of quantum information?”** arXiv:2504.05998; Phys. Rev. D 113, L021905 (2026).

They map gravitational interaction between optomechanical systems to an optical communication channel and explicitly use non-entanglement-breaking behavior as a criterion for quantum gravitational information transmission.

In their quadratic-gravity model the effective map is a Gaussian thermal attenuator with a sharp EB/non-EB transition controlled by thermal noise.

## Verdict

Do not claim novelty for

- framing gravitational interaction as a quantum communication channel;
- using the EB boundary as the definition of whether gravity can preserve quantum information;
- Gaussian thermal-attenuator descriptions of gravitational information transfer;
- a sharp thermal EB/nonclassical transition.

This remains true even though their interaction is near-field/Newtonian rather than the repository's source-resolved radiative link.

# 6. Existing gravity-mediated entanglement and causality literature

Several earlier and contemporary results remove additional broad claims from consideration.

## Belenchia et al. 2018 / 2019

The Alice/Bob massive-superposition gedankenexperiments explicitly address

- center-of-mass conservation;
- gravitational radiation;
- vacuum fluctuations;
- causality/complementarity;
- quantum information carried by the gravitational field of a superposition.

Therefore neither “gravity carries branch information” nor “conservation must be included to avoid false gravitational conclusions” is new.

## Danielson–Satishchandran–Wald 2021

Their rigorous treatment connects gravitationally mediated entanglement with direct entanglement between the source and on-shell gravitons and analyzes the associated decoherence.

Thus source–graviton entanglement and its relation to Newtonian-looking gravitational entanglement are established concepts.

## Relativistic 2026 gravity-entanglement work

Recent linearized-gravity analyses also make relativistic retardation and causal local mediation explicit. Therefore “including retardation in gravity-mediated entanglement” is not itself a novelty claim.
