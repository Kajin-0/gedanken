# 2. Abstract — recommended direction

We construct a source-resolved weak-gravity quantum link from local preparation to a distant noisy receiver. A degenerate reference qubit and a branch-common work mode prepare opposite coherent amplitudes of a finite-mass four-spoke elastic quadrupole through an internal sign-controlled interaction. The support and controller are included in the local conserved source, whose branch-difference quadrupole and graviton linewidth can be calculated analytically. All radiation emitted during preparation and subsequent source evolution is compressed into a normalized gravitational temporal mode. Retarded free-space propagation and resonant receiver input-output theory then reduce the ideal vacuum link to four dimensionless factors: source gravitational branching, free-space/source-mode capture, receiver gravitational branching, and temporal loading. For aligned plus-quadrupole modes in the compact-source wave zone,

$$
\tau_{A\to B}(t)
=
\frac{\kappa_{g,A}}{\kappa_A}
\frac{25\mathcal O}{16(kR)^2}
\frac{\kappa_{g,B}}{\kappa_B}
\mathcal T_f(t),
\qquad
0\le\mathcal T_f\le1.
$$

Established Gaussian-channel results then imply that the locally prepared binary-coherent source-reference probe remains NPT after reception precisely when the resulting coherent parameter exceeds the accumulated channel noise. The factorization separates physical interface quality from waveform engineering: a matched passive exponential gives \(\mathcal T_{\max}=4e^{-2}\), a smooth \(\sin^4\) source gives about \(0.798\), and ideal target-time shaping can approach unity, but none can overcome poor source or receiver gravitational branching. We derive passive-class bounds, finite-source and reciprocal-feedback corrections, and show that a deliberately aggressive kilogram–meter–MHz benchmark falls from a receiver-local \(10^{-22}\) scale to an end-to-end \(10^{-42}\) scale when ordinary source branching is included. The construction is a theoretical normalization benchmark, not a near-term experimental proposal.
