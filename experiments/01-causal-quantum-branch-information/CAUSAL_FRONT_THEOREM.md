## 1. Model assumptions

The theorem is intentionally explicit and narrow.

1. **Source branch qubit.** The source has balanced alternatives $|L\rangle,|R\rangle$.
2. **Finite coherent branch encoding.** The source branch difference is encoded in one normalized incoming bosonic mode as
   $$
   |\Psi_a\rangle
   =\frac{|L\rangle|a\rangle+|R\rangle|-a\rangle}{\sqrt2},
   \qquad 0<|a|<\infty.
   $$
3. **Relativistic causality.** The source-controlled incoming mode has no support at the receiver before $t_0=R/c$.
4. **Linear passive Markov receiver.** One receiver mode $c$ obeys
   $$
   \dot c
   =-\frac{\kappa_{\rm tot}}2c
   +\sqrt{\kappa_\Delta}\,b_\Delta^{\rm in}
   +\sum_a\sqrt{\kappa_a}\,b_a^{\rm in},
   $$
   where $\kappa_\Delta$ is the coupling to the source-matched branch-difference mode and the $a$ ports are uncontrolled passive channels.
5. **Gaussian thermal uncontrolled ports.** Port $a$ has mean occupation $\bar n_a$.
6. **Stationary thermal receiver before arrival.** The receiver has reached the stationary branch-independent thermal floor generated by the uncontrolled ports.
7. **Normalized source wavepacket.** The incoming difference-mode temporal envelope satisfies
   $$
   \int_0^\infty d\tau\,|f(\tau)|^2=1.
   $$
8. **Phase-insensitive single-mode reduced channel.** At every fixed receiver time, the map from the selected incoming source mode to the stored receiver mode is a thermal attenuator characterized by coherent transfer coefficient $\eta_f(t)$ and branch-independent output thermal occupation $m(t)$.

Define

$$
\kappa_{\rm tot}
=\kappa_\Delta+\sum_a\kappa_a,
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
\Gamma_{\rm th}
=\sum_a\bar n_a\kappa_a.
}
$$

## 2. Exact finite-cat lemma

For a thermal attenuator with coherent transmissivity $\eta$ and output thermal occupation $m$, the hybrid source-cat state

$$
\frac{|L\rangle|a\rangle+|R\rangle|-a\rangle}{\sqrt2}
$$

has an NPT source-receiver output for **every finite $a\neq0$** iff

$$
\boxed{\eta>m.}
$$

For a conventional attenuator with environmental occupation $\bar n$,

$$
m=(1-\eta)\bar n,
$$

so this is equivalent to the standard non-entanglement-breaking condition

$$
\boxed{
\eta>\frac{\bar n}{\bar n+1}.
}
$$

A full proof is given in `EXACT_FINITE_CAT_THERMAL_THEOREM.md`.

This lemma removes the weak-cat assumption from the causal-front result.

## 3. Theorem

### Exact causal quantum-front speed limit for finite coherent cats

Under assumptions 1--8, no normalized source branch-difference wavepacket can produce source-receiver NPT entanglement before

$$
\boxed{
T_{\rm NPT}^{\min}
=
\frac Rc+
\frac1{\kappa_{\rm tot}}
\ln\left[
\frac{\kappa_\Delta}
{\kappa_\Delta-\Gamma_{\rm th}}
\right]
}
$$

provided

$$
\boxed{
\kappa_\Delta>\Gamma_{\rm th}.
}
$$

If

$$
\boxed{
\kappa_\Delta\le\Gamma_{\rm th},
}
$$

then **no finite coherent source cat** can generate an NPT source-receiver state at any finite time within the model.

The lower bound is tight: for every admissible parameter set above threshold, a time-reversed receiver-matched input waveform saturates the coherent-capture inequality and reaches the NPT boundary at the stated time.

The sign boundary is independent of cat amplitude $a$. Cat amplitude controls the magnitude and observability of the resulting entanglement, not the location of the NPT front.

## 4. Proof

Measure time after causal arrival,

$$
\tau=t-R/c.
$$

The source-controlled receiver amplitude is proportional to

$$
\sqrt{\kappa_\Delta}
\int_0^\tau ds\,
e^{-\kappa_{\rm tot}(\tau-s)/2}f(s).
$$

Define the coherent transfer coefficient

$$
\boxed{
\eta_f(\tau)
=\kappa_\Delta
\left|
\int_0^\tau ds\,
e^{-\kappa_{\rm tot}(\tau-s)/2}f(s)
\right|^2.
}
$$

By Cauchy--Schwarz,

$$
\eta_f(\tau)
\le
\kappa_\Delta
\left[
\int_0^\tau ds\,
e^{-\kappa_{\rm tot}(\tau-s)}
\right]
\left[
\int_0^\tau ds\,|f(s)|^2
\right].
$$

Because the complete incoming source mode is normalized,

$$
\int_0^\tau ds\,|f(s)|^2\le1.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{
\eta_f(\tau)
\le
\frac{\kappa_\Delta}{\kappa_{\rm tot}}
\left(1-e^{-\kappa_{\rm tot}\tau}\right).
}
$$

For the stationary thermal receiver,

$$
\boxed{
 m_*
=\frac{\Gamma_{\rm th}}{\kappa_{\rm tot}}.
}
$$

By the exact finite-cat thermal lemma, for every finite $a\neq0$ the source-receiver state is NPT at time $\tau$ iff

$$
\eta_f(\tau)>m_*.
$$

Therefore a necessary condition at time $\tau$ is

$$
\kappa_\Delta
\left(1-e^{-\kappa_{\rm tot}\tau}\right)
>\Gamma_{\rm th}.
$$

If

$$
\kappa_\Delta\le\Gamma_{\rm th},
$$

the inequality can never be satisfied, proving the no-front result for every finite coherent cat.

If

$$
\kappa_\Delta>\Gamma_{\rm th},
$$

then

$$
e^{-\kappa_{\rm tot}\tau}
<1-\frac{\Gamma_{\rm th}}{\kappa_\Delta},
$$

and therefore

$$
\tau>
\frac1{\kappa_{\rm tot}}
\ln\left[
\frac{\kappa_\Delta}
{\kappa_\Delta-\Gamma_{\rm th}}
\right].
$$

Adding the light-cone delay $R/c$ proves the lower bound.

## 5. Saturating waveform

At a chosen target time $\tau_*$, equality in Cauchy--Schwarz is achieved by

$$
\boxed{
f_{\rm opt}(s)
=
\sqrt{
\frac{\kappa_{\rm tot}}
{1-e^{-\kappa_{\rm tot}\tau_*}}
}
\,
e^{-\kappa_{\rm tot}(\tau_*-s)/2}
\Theta(s)\Theta(\tau_*-s).
}
$$

This is the time reverse of the receiver's free ringdown over the available interval and satisfies

$$
\int_0^{\tau_*}ds\,|f_{\rm opt}(s)|^2=1.
$$

Thus the causal front bound is tight within the ideal receiver model.

## 6. Quantum excess fraction

Define

$$
\boxed{
\epsilon_Q
=1-
\frac{\Gamma_{\rm th}}
{\kappa_\Delta}.
}
$$

For a quantum-capable receiver,

$$
0<\epsilon_Q\le1.
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{
T_{\rm NPT}^{\min}-R/c
=-\frac1{\kappa_{\rm tot}}\ln\epsilon_Q.
}
$$

The earliest possible source-receiver entanglement front therefore contains two distinct delays:

1. propagation delay $R/c$;
2. quantum build time $-\kappa_{\rm tot}^{-1}\ln\epsilon_Q$.

## 7. Corollary: exact finite-cat double critical slowing

As the receiver approaches the entanglement-breaking boundary,

$$
\epsilon_Q\to0^+,
$$

so

$$
\boxed{
T_{\rm NPT}^{\min}-R/c\to\infty
}
$$

logarithmically.

At the saturating front,

$$
\frac{d\eta_{\max}}{d\tau}
=\kappa_\Delta e^{-\kappa_{\rm tot}\tau},
$$

so

$$
\boxed{
\left.
\frac{d\eta_{\max}}{d\tau}
\right|_{\tau_*}
=\kappa_\Delta\epsilon_Q
=\kappa_\Delta-\Gamma_{\rm th}.
}
$$

The front therefore both retreats and becomes dynamically weak as the thermal classicalization boundary is approached.

The exact finite-cat lemma shows that the **location of this transition does not move when the source cat amplitude changes**.

## 9. What the theorem does NOT say

The theorem is not a universal theorem of quantum gravity.

It does **not** automatically cover:

- non-Markovian receivers;
- active/inverted receivers;
- strongly nonlinear capture;
- phase-sensitive or non-Gaussian noise channels;
- arbitrary multimode branch encodings that cannot be reduced to one captured difference mode plus branch-independent complements;
- pre-existing source-receiver correlations;
- gravitational dressing subtleties not captured by the operational source/receiver channel description.

It also does not assert that entanglement is a locally propagating substance. The causal statement concerns the response to a controlled source operation and a receiver input channel with no source-dependent support before $R/c$.
